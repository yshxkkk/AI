\textbf{Purpose.} This document contains the complete point--region--point definitions, assumptions, proofs and implementation contracts used by the main text. The central scientific object is a finite-resolution probability record with a declared allocation, effective support, geometry, uncertainty, refinement state and action contract. Its statistical guarantees are conditional on the stated sampling, selection, representation, transport and perturbation assumptions; its computation state is reusable only within a frozen allocation contract; support, boundaries and failure triggers are auditable; and stability must be checked by assignment-preserving or reassignment-aware perturbation audits. A region is therefore a probability object with an explicit contract, not a privileged clustering method. Where an empirical audit uses MiniBatchKMeans centres, the resulting object is labelled a regional allocation cell; Granular Ball denotes the interface-level bounded envelope and is not a claim that MiniBatchKMeans is a distinct Granular Ball optimizer. The SI supplies the definitions, assumptions, proofs, algorithms and theorem-to-experiment map needed to audit the regional computational layer. Every operative theorem statement and proof is carried here as part of this manuscript package. The new empirical tables are generated by the scripts and manifests named in S14--S18 and S22. The published EEG interval is a Clopper--Pearson count interval for the declared regional estimand; it is not a fixed-$x$ or dependence-robust coverage theorem.

\section{Probability objects, targets and sampling roles}\label{sec:setup}
Let $(X,Y)$ take values in a standard Borel space $\mathcal X\times\mathcal Y$. Unless another range is stated, the response score $Z=\psi(Y)$ lies in $[0,1]$. This includes an indicator for a class; bounded regression can be rescaled. All conditional expectations are versions of regular conditional laws. A value at a particular covariate requires a specified continuous or otherwise identified version.

Write $\mathcal S$ for all construction information. It can include encoder training, candidate generation, pilots and hyperparameter proposals. Validation observations $D_V$, estimation observations $D_E$, independent refit observations $D_R$, and calibration anchors $D_C$ have separate roles. A result that conditions on $\mathcal S$ uses observations independent of that information. Conditional statements are understood almost surely in $\mathcal S$; failure probabilities for additional construction events are added explicitly.

\begin{definition}[Regional probability space]\label{def:region}
A frozen representation $\phi:\mathcal X\to\R^d$ and measurable allocation $a:\R^d\to\{1,\ldots,K\}$ define raw-input cells
\[
 A_j=\{x:a(\phi(x))=j\},\qquad
 \pi_j=P_X(A_j),\qquad t_j=\E[Z\mid X\in A_j]\quad(\pi_j>0).
\]
The allocation cells form a partition modulo null sets. A nominal envelope $B_j$ contains their represented support and can be spherical, ellipsoidal or another bounded set. Overlapping envelopes are resolved by a fixed tie rule. The induced sigma-field is
$\mathcal F_\Pi=\sigma(A_1,\ldots,A_K)$ and
$t_\Pi(X)=\sum_jt_j\ind_{A_j}(X)=\E[Z\mid\mathcal F_\Pi]$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Seven-module frozen regional probability contract]\label{def:sevencontract}
Let $D_S,D_C,D_V,D_E,D_T$ denote construction, calibration, validation, estimation/refit and outer-test roles. A frozen contract is
\[
\mathfrak C=\bigl(\phi,\Pi,\{B_j,\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy},\widehat p_j,\widehat\theta_{j,D},G_j,F_j\}_{j=1}^K,\mathcal H,\mathcal G,\mathcal A,\mathcal T\bigr),
\]
where $\phi$ is the declared representation, $\Pi=\{A_j\}$ is a covariate-only raw-space allocation with a fixed tie rule, $B_j=\phi(A_j)$ is its recorded representation-space envelope, $\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}$ is the normalized push-forward of the target deployment measure conditional on $A_j$ (with region mass $M_{j,D}=\Pr_D\{X\in A_j\}$ recorded separately when needed), $\widehat p_j$ is an optional conditional probability surface, and
\[
\widehat\theta_{j,D}=\int_{B_j}\widehat p_j(z)\,d\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}(z)
\]
is the regional estimate. All displayed integrals over $B_j$ use this push-forward measure; the equivalent raw-space form integrates over $A_j$. $G_j$ records support, posterior width, regret, boundary and stability certificates; $F_j$ records failure reason and fallback; $\mathcal H$, $\mathcal G$, $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal T$ are, respectively, optional parent--child refinement, region-graph, action-cost and multi-horizon operators. A module may be disabled and represented by its identity map. No deployed operator may read a query outcome label. The target is
\[
\theta_{j,D}=\int_{B_j}m_D(z)\,d\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}(z),\qquad m_D(z)=\E_D[Y\mid\phi(X)=z].
\]
Thus $\widehat p_j(z)$ is an estimated query surface and $\theta_{j,D}$ is a deployment regional action estimand; they are recorded separately. Under an invariant conditional-law transport assumption, $m_D=m_S=m_\phi$ and the source representation surface can be transported. If that assumption is not justified, the target remains a sensitivity or fallback quantity rather than an identified deployment probability.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Notation map for the two estimands.}
$A_j$ is a frozen, covariate-only raw-space allocation cell and $B_j=\phi(A_j)$ its representation-space envelope. The symbols $n_{j,\mathrm{eff}}$ and $n_{j,\mathrm{eff}}^{\mathrm{bal}}$ denote effective and class-balanced support; support is never denoted by $q_j$. The true conditional surface is $m_D(z)=\E_D[Y\mid\phi(X)=z]$ for the declared source or deployment law $D$, while $\widehat q_j(z)$ or $\widehat m_j(z)$ denotes its fitted sample-level surface and is evaluated with sample-level Brier and ECE. The source regional mean is $\eta_{j,S}=\E_S[Y\mid X\in A_j]$. The deployment action estimand is $\theta_{j,D}=\int_{A_j}m_D(x)\,d\mu_{j,D}^{\mathrm{deploy}}(x)$, equivalently the displayed push-forward integral over $B_j$, and its estimator is $\widehat\theta_{j,D}=\int_{A_j}\widehat q_j(x)\,d\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\mathrm{deploy}}(x)$. In the Solar tables, $p_{A,S}^{(h)}$ is a source-table description and does not replace $\theta_{A,D}^{(h)}$. For raw-space notation below, $m_D(x)$ abbreviates $m_D(\phi(x))$ and $\mu_{j,D}^{X,\mathrm{deploy}}$ denotes the raw-space conditional measure; formulas over $B_j$ use the push-forward $\mu_{j,D}^{\mathrm{deploy}}$. In the zero-radius theorem, $\delta_x$ denotes $\delta_{\phi(x)}$.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional regional integration and fail-closed routing]\label{prop:regionalcontract}
Suppose the allocation, envelopes and tie rule are frozen before the outer test role, and an accepted cell satisfies
\[
\sup_{z\in B_j}|\widehat p_j(z)-m_D(z)|\le\varepsilon_{p,j},
\qquad
\left|\int f\,d\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}-\int f\,d\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}\right|\le\varepsilon_{\mu,j}
\]
for $f=m_D$ on the declared integration class. Then, with the same event probability as the two displayed certificates,
\[
|\widehat\theta_{j,D}-\theta_{j,D}|\le\varepsilon_{p,j}+\varepsilon_{\mu,j}.
\]
For a binary response and a constant regional output, the conditional Brier risks under the declared deployment measure satisfy
\[
R_{{\rm Br},D}(\widehat\theta_{j,D}\mid B_j)-R_{{\rm Br},D}(\theta_{j,D}\mid B_j)
=(\widehat\theta_{j,D}-\theta_{j,D})^2,
\]
and relative to the point oracle $m_D$ the excess is
\[
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}}\{m_D(Z)\mid B_j\}+(\widehat\theta_{j,D}-\theta_{j,D})^2.
\]
If $G_j$ fails, the contract outputs the declared point predictor, parent object or abstention in $F_j$; the proposition therefore certifies the regional estimand on accepted cells but does not imply regional dominance over the point oracle.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Add and subtract $\int m_D\,d\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}$ and apply the two certificate bounds. The Brier identities follow by expanding the target-measure expectation of $(q-Y)^2$ around $m_D$ and then around its target conditional mean $\theta_{j,D}$. The fallback statement is definitional and preserves the allocation record.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Joint surface--measure certificate]\label{def:jointmeasure}
For an accepted region $B_j$, let $\widehat p_j$ be a conditional probability surface and let $\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}$ be an estimated target deployment measure. Define the surface and transport remainders
\[
 e_{p,j}=\sup_{z\in B_j}|\widehat p_j(z)-m_D(z)|,
 \qquad
 e_{\mu,j}=\left|\int_{B_j}m_D\,d\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}-
                         \int_{B_j}m_D\,d\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}\right| .
\]
A joint certificate stores upper bounds for both remainders, their calibration roles and the resampling unit. When the same observations estimate both terms, the certificate also stores a paired upper bound $u_{\mathrm{joint},j}$ for their sum; marginal quantiles are not added as if they were independent.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Joint surface--measure integration bound]\label{prop:jointmeasure}
Suppose that, before the outer test role, the allocation and envelopes are frozen and the joint certificate satisfies
\[
 \Pr\{e_{p,j}\le u_{p,j},\ e_{\mu,j}\le u_{\mu,j}\}\ge 1-\delta_j .
\]
Then on the same event,
\[
 \left|\int_{B_j}\widehat p_j\,d\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}-
        \int_{B_j}m_D\,d\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}\right|
 \le u_{p,j}+u_{\mu,j} .
\]
When both remainders use common data, any proposed bound on their sum must preserve this dependence and independently satisfy a valid coverage event. A paired bootstrap quantile alone does not establish that event: the oracle surface is unknown and nuisance bias, selection and group dependence require justification. The following deterministic implication is conditional on the stated event, not a bootstrap coverage theorem. For a binary response and a constant regional report, the target-measure excess Brier risk relative to the point oracle is bounded on this event by
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}}\{m_D(Z)\mid B_j\}+
 (u_{p,j}+u_{\mu,j})^2 .
\]
A failed support, posterior-width or measure certificate sets the local surface weight to zero and invokes the declared parent or point fallback.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Add and subtract $\int m_D\,d\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}$ and use that $\widehat\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy}$ is a probability measure to bound the first term by $e_{p,j}$. The second term is $e_{\mu,j}$. The Brier identity follows by the conditional-variance decomposition. No resampling validity follows from this algebraic identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Resolution-gated nested Logistic head]
Let $r\geq0$ be a declared probability-resolution parameter, let
$\eta_{\rm pt,\beta}(z)=\beta_0+\beta^\top\widetilde z$ be the fixed point Logistic
logit, and let $H_{r,\gamma}$ be a bounded local residual with
$H_{0,\gamma}\equiv0$ and with the null local head $H_{r,0}\equiv0$ for every
$r\geq0$. Thus the positive-resolution family contains the point Logistic
submodel obtained by setting $\gamma=0$. Define
\[
 \eta_{r,\beta,\gamma}(z)=\eta_{\rm pt,\beta}(z)+rH_{r,\gamma}(z),
 \qquad q_{r,\beta,\gamma}(z)=\sigma\{\eta_{r,\beta,\gamma}(z)\}.
\]
The zero-resolution contract is an identity allocation on the represented query
set: $A_{x,0}=\{x\}$, $\mu^{\rm deploy}_{x,0}=\delta_x$, duplicate queries use the
fixed tie rule without multiplicity reweighting, and the local block is disabled.
For a fixed design and a shared logistic-loss convention (clipped or unclipped), write
\[
 L_r(\beta,\gamma)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_{\log}
 (Y_i,q_{r,\beta,\gamma}(Z_i))
 +\lambda_\beta\|\beta\|_2^2+\lambda_\gamma\|\gamma\|_2^2,
 \qquad \lambda_\gamma>0,\qquad
 L_{\rm pt}(\beta):=L_0(\beta,0).
\]
The equality theorem uses the same convention on both sides. The released sklearn fit uses its
standard unclipped training loss and applies clipping only to reported proper scores; the
identity verifier therefore treats the objective as an abstract shared-convention identity
and separately checks the reported prediction functionals.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Zero-radius point closure]
\label{thm:zero_radius_point_closure}
Assume the same represented design, query set, regularization convention and
outcome--prediction pairs are used for the point and regional objectives, and
that the point Logistic minimizer is unique or selected by the same deterministic
tie rule. Under the zero-resolution contract,
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\eta_{0,\widehat\beta,\gamma}=\eta_{\rm pt,\widehat\beta}$ and
$q_{0,\widehat\beta,\gamma}=q_{\rm pt}$ for every $\gamma$;
\item $\inf_\gamma L_0(\beta,\gamma)=L_{\rm pt}(\beta)$, the canonical
minimizer is $(\widehat\beta_{\rm pt},0)$, and the optimized objective value is
exactly the point Logistic objective value;
\item the regional target is a point target,
$\theta_{x,0}=\int m_D(z)d\delta_x(z)=m_D(x)$, while its estimator is
$\widehat\theta_{x,0}=\int q_{0}(z)d\delta_x(z)=q_{\rm pt}(x)$. Thus the
source/deployment target and the point Logistic estimate retain their ordinary
point meanings on the same query set;
\item every calibration functional computed from the identical prediction--outcome
pairs, including Brier, clipped log loss, ECE and their paired intervals, is equal
to its point Logistic value. Coverage for the true point target remains subject
to the usual point-model validity conditions; zero-radius closure does not create
coverage by itself; and
\item every deterministic threshold, ranking, fixed-budget selection, abstention
or action utility decision is equal to the point decision, provided the deterministic utility reads only the same prediction, threshold, budget and abstention state and not region metadata. If a positive-radius
gate is unavailable, its fail-closed map is the point route, so rejection also
preserves this equality.
\end{enumerate}
This is an exact semantic and optimization containment result. Merely recording a
zero geometric radius while retaining a multi-observation cell does not satisfy
the identity allocation and does not imply point recovery.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Because $H_{0,\gamma}\equiv0$, the logit and all predictions equal the point
logit. Substitution in $L_0$ leaves the point objective plus
$\lambda_\gamma\|\gamma\|_2^2$, whose unique canonical minimizer is $\gamma=0$;
minimizing over $\beta$ therefore gives the point solution. The singleton-measure
identity $\int f\,d\delta_x=f(x)$ proves the target equality. All remaining
claims are equality of functionals evaluated on the same prediction--outcome
pairs and the stipulated identity action/fallback map.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Remark on the point limit.}
The theorem concerns the resolution operator, not a numerical radius field in
isolation. Shrinking the recorded radius of an existing multi-point allocation
to zero while retaining shared membership is a different model and need not
recover point Logistic.

\begin{theorem}[Conditional finite-sample certificate sufficiency and selective risk]
\label{thm:certificate_sufficiency}
Let a finite menu contain at most $M$ candidate allocations/heads and at most
$K$ cells. Conditional on the construction record, use disjoint independent
roles for estimation, certificate validation, deployment-measure estimation and
outer testing; if observations are serially dependent, the theorem applies to
predeclared independent blocks whose bounded block averages define
$n^{\rm eff}_j$. Before validation, the representation, covariate-only
allocation, metric, tie rule and candidate menu are frozen. Let
$P_{V,j}$ be the validation law of $(X,Y)$ conditional on $X\in A_j$, and let
$P_{D,j}$ be the deployment law under the same frozen raw-space allocation.
The deployment measure is
$\mu^{\rm deploy}_{j,D}=\mathcal L_D(\phi(X)\mid X\in A_j)$ and its validation
analogue is $\mu^{\rm deploy}_{j,V}=\mathcal L_V(\phi(X)\mid X\in A_j)$; both
are estimated only from their separately declared measure roles. For an accepted cell
$A_j$, assume the following event $\mathcal G$ holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(A\arabic*)]
\item target support satisfies $\pi_{j,D}=P_D(A_j)\geq\underline\pi_j>0$;
 the validation record contains $n^{\rm eff}_j\geq n_{\min}$ independent
 blocks, and the independent measure role has the declared effective support;
\item the target conditional surface is homogeneous on the accepted envelope,
$\sup_{x\in A_j}|m_D(x)-\theta_{j,D}|\leq h_j$. If
$|m_D(x)-m_D(x')|\leq L_j\|\phi(x)-\phi(x')\|^\alpha$ on a ball of
radius $\rho_j$ and $\theta_{j,D}$ is its deployment conditional mean, then
$h_j\leq 2^\alpha L_j\rho_j^\alpha$;
\item validation-to-deployment drift is bounded jointly for the response and
representation laws,
$\operatorname{TV}(P_{V,j},P_{D,j})\leq\Delta_j$ and
$\operatorname{TV}(\mu_{j,V}^{\rm deploy},\mu_{j,D}^{\rm deploy})\leq\Delta^{\mu}_j$,
where throughout this theorem
$\operatorname{TV}(P,Q)=\sup_{0\leq f\leq1}|\E_Pf-\E_Qf|$;
\item let $A'_j$ denote the perturbed or reassigned version of $A_j$ under the declared
assignment audit, with $P_D(A_j\triangle A'_j)\leq b_j<\underline\pi_j$. For $[0,1]$-valued
regional means set $d^{(1)}_{{\rm bdry},j}=2b_j/(\underline\pi_j-b_j)$;
for a signed paired loss in $[-1,1]$ set
$d^{(\pm)}_{{\rm bdry},j}=4b_j/(\underline\pi_j-b_j)$;
\item an independent joint certificate, simultaneous over the $KM$ candidate-cell
pairs, satisfies
$\Pr(e_{p,j}\leq u_{p,j},e_{\mu,j}\leq u_{\mu,j}\mid\mathcal G)\geq 1-\delta_{\rm joint}$, or these terms are zero for an independently estimated
constant regional mean. The structural event itself is required to satisfy
$\Pr(\mathcal G)\geq1-\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}
Let $\delta_{\rm stat}$ be the simultaneous independent-block concentration
budget and set $\delta_*=\delta_{\rm stat}+\delta_{\rm joint}$. Define
\[
 \epsilon_j=\sqrt{\frac{\log(2KM/\delta_{\rm stat})}{2n^{\rm eff}_j}},\qquad
 \varepsilon_j=\epsilon_j+h_j+\Delta_j+\Delta^{\mu}_j
 +d^{(1)}_{{\rm bdry},j}+u_{p,j}+u_{\mu,j}.
\]
If the support, homogeneity, boundary, drift and calibration gates pass, then,
conditional on $\mathcal G$, with probability at least $1-\delta_*$
simultaneously over every accepted cell and candidate,
\[
 |\widehat\theta_{j,D}-\theta_{j,D}|\leq\varepsilon_j,
 \qquad I_j=[\widehat\theta_{j,D}-\varepsilon_j,
             \widehat\theta_{j,D}+\varepsilon_j]\cap[0,1]
\]
has regional target coverage at least $1-\delta_*$. Unconditionally the
coverage is at least $1-\gamma-\delta_*$. For binary Brier risk of a
constant regional action, the same event gives
\[
 R_{\rm Br,D}(\widehat\theta_j\mid A_j)-R_{\rm Br,D}(m_D\mid A_j)
 \leq (h_j+\varepsilon_j)^2.
\]
This is coverage for the regional estimand, not fixed-$x$ coverage for a
sample-level surface.

For selective use, let $D_{ji}$ be an independent-block paired validation loss
difference between the regional candidate and the point Logistic predictor,
$D_{ji}\in[-1,1]$. Let
\[
 \widehat d_j=n_j^{-1}\sum_iD_{ji},\qquad
 r_j=\sqrt{\frac{2\log(2KM/\delta_{\rm stat})}{n_j}},\qquad
 U_j=\widehat d_j+r_j+2\Delta_j+d^{(\pm)}_{{\rm bdry},j}.
\]
The factor $2$ in both drift and boundary transfer is required because the
paired loss difference is signed and lies in $[-1,1]$ under the displayed TV
convention. If a cell is accepted only when $U_j\leq0$ in addition to the
preceding gates, then the fail-closed route $Q$ satisfies
\[
 R_D(Q)-R_D(q_{\rm pt})
 =\sum_j\pi_{j,D}G_j\{R_{D,j}(\widehat q_j)-R_{D,j}(q_{\rm pt})\}
 \leq\sum_j\pi_{j,D}G_jU_j\leq0
\]
with conditional probability at least $1-\delta_*$ and unconditional
probability at least $1-\gamma-\delta_*$. Thus certificate passage can imply
a finite-sample regional interval and selective non-inferiority, but never an
unconditional regional gain. If any assumption or gate fails, the object is
refined, returned to point Logistic or rejected.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditionally on $\mathcal G$ and the frozen construction record, Hoeffding's
inequality for independent blocks and a union bound over at most $KM$
candidate-cell pairs give the statistical term $\epsilon_j$ for bounded
$[0,1]$ regional responses. The homogeneity assumption bounds the difference
between a regional mean and any target point by $h_j$; the stated Holder
specialization uses the diameter $2\rho_j$, hence the factor $2^\alpha$.
The two displayed TV assumptions transport both the response and deployment
measure terms. The symmetric-difference audit changes a normalized
$[0,1]$ cell expectation by at most $d^{(1)}_{{\rm bdry},j}$. The surface and
measure remainders are controlled jointly by the $\delta_{\rm joint}$ event.
A union bound gives $\delta_*$. The Brier decomposition in
Proposition~\ref{prop:regionalcontract} and
$|m_D-\widehat\theta_j|\leq h_j+\varepsilon_j$ give the risk bound.
For $D_{ji}\in[-1,1]$, Hoeffding has range length $2$ and therefore gives
$r_j=\sqrt{2\log(2KM/\delta_{\rm stat})/n_j}$. Under the displayed TV
convention, a signed $[-1,1]$ loss transfers with factor $2\Delta_j$ and the
boundary replacement with factor $d^{(\pm)}_{{\rm bdry},j}$.
Applying the same block argument and taking a union bound proves the paired
regret gate; linearity of target risk over accepted cells proves the selective
bound. The unconditional statement follows by subtracting the structural
failure probability $\gamma$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Support as a prior rather than a post-hoc gate.}
A support-conditioned local correction can be written as
\[
 \widehat p_j(x)=\sigma\!\left(\ell_0(x)+\lambda_j r_j(x)\right),
 \qquad
 \lambda_j=\frac{n_{\mathrm{eff},j}}
 {n_{\mathrm{eff},j}+\tau_j^2},
\]
where $r_j$ is an out-of-fold residual surface, $n_{\mathrm{eff},j}$ is class-balanced or dependence-adjusted support, and $\tau_j^2$ is selected on calibration data. Small-support regions therefore shrink continuously toward the parent or point surface before the certificate is evaluated. The certificate remains fail-closed: a residual is not promoted simply because its prior weight is nonzero.

\paragraph{Two-layer construction and task-conditioned quality.}
The allocation layer specifies which represented observations share a cell ID; the quality layer specifies whether that cell is adequate for a declared task. The allocation may be learned from labelled or unlabelled construction data, but a label-informed construction must be included in the declared structure role. After freezing, $a_{\mathcal S}(\phi_{\mathcal S}(x))$ is a measurable function of covariates alone and can be reused without a query outcome label; its reuse contract is nevertheless tied to the declared task, label definition and allocation fingerprint. The second layer uses calibration or validation responses to record effective support, class-balanced support, purity or posterior width, within-cell response heterogeneity, proper-score regret relative to the point model, and the action or abstention status. Thus labels need not define every geometric centre, but they are part of the regional computational object whenever the task is supervised. For a binary task, a useful purity diagnostic is
\[
\operatorname{purity}(A)=\max\{\Pr(Y=1\mid A),1-\Pr(Y=1\mid A)\},
\]
but purity alone is not a sufficient quality certificate: a highly pure cell can still be unsupported, overconfident, or worse than the point model under a proper score. A multiclass cell stores the full class-count vector rather than only its modal label. The splitting rule is therefore model- and task-conditioned: refine a cell when its current regional model fails support, width, calibration, regret, boundary, or action criteria, or when a child passes a predeclared improvement test. A difference in response means is only a candidate heterogeneity signal, not the definition of a valid split. A class may occupy multiple cells, and the number of cells is not constrained to the number of classes.

The equivalence relation belongs to the allocation map, not to the visual shape of an envelope: $x\sim_\Pi x'$ iff $a(\phi(x))=a(\phi(x'))$. A spherical Granular Ball is the canonical isotropic representation relative to the declared norm; an $\ell_1$ ball is isotropic in its own norm even though it is not a Euclidean sphere. Diagonal, low-rank and tree envelopes are recorded as non-standard shape extensions and sensitivity controls. They inherit the regional interface only after the same fixed tie rule, support record, uncertainty calculation and failure contract are supplied; they are not silently treated as equivalent to the canonical ball.

The centre $c_j$ lies in representation space. A support radius is
$\rho_j=\mathop{\rm ess\,sup}_{X\in A_j}\|\phi(X)-c_j\|$.
When an older formula below writes $m(c_A)$ or $q(c_A)$, it presupposes a
specified raw representative $x_A$ with $\phi(x_A)=c_A$, and denotes $m(x_A)$ or
$q(x_A)$. If no such representative is supplied, those point-centred formulas
are inapplicable; use $g(c_A)$ for the represented centre or the regional target.
An empirical maximum is a descriptive radius and is not a bound on unobserved support. A semidefinite quadratic form can have unbounded null directions; it alone does not imply a finite Euclidean support radius.

\textbf{Three estimands.} Regional confidence concerns $t_A=\E[Z\mid A]$. Point confidence concerns $m(x)=\E[Z\mid X=x]$. Prediction concerns a future observed $Y^\star$. These require different arguments. For grouped data we also distinguish row-weighted and group-balanced targets; the latter is defined in \Sref{sec:groups}.

\textbf{Measurability convention.} Candidate menus are finite or countable, or have a measurable separable version for the stated suprema. A deterministic tie rule selects a minimizer. Optimization tolerances compare the actual objective to a declared comparator class; local greedy gains alone do not provide a global certificate.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional and unconditional validity]\label{prop:tower}
If a random interval $I(D_E,\mathcal S)$ satisfies
$\Pp\{t_A(\mathcal S)\in I\mid\mathcal S\}\ge1-\delta$ almost surely, then its unconditional coverage is at least $1-\delta$. If this holds only on a construction event $G$ with $\Pp(G)\ge1-\gamma$, its unconditional coverage is at least $1-\delta-\gamma$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integrate the conditional failure probability. On $G$ it is at most $\delta$, and on $G^c$ it is at most one. Thus the total failure probability is at most $\delta+\gamma$. This also shows why conditioning for a proof does not mean that the final coverage probability must remain conditional.
\end{proof}

\section{Refinement, representation loss and reusable summaries}\label{sec:projection}
\begin{theorem}[Projection identity and the correct point limit]\label{thm:projection}
For $Z\in L_2$, if $\mathcal F_\Pi\subseteq\mathcal F_{\Pi'}$, then
\[
 \E[(Z-t_\Pi)^2]-\E[(Z-t_{\Pi'})^2]=\E[(t_{\Pi'}-t_\Pi)^2]\ge0.
\]
For nested partitions, $t_{\Pi_\ell}$ converges in $L_2$ to
$\E[Z\mid\mathcal F_\infty]$, where $\mathcal F_\infty=\sigma(\bigcup_\ell\mathcal F_{\Pi_\ell})$.
If this is $\sigma(\phi(X))$, the limit is $m_\phi(\phi(X))=\E[Z\mid\phi(X)]$.
It equals $m(X)$ only if $m(X)$ is $\sigma(\phi(X))$-measurable.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The tower property gives $\E[t_{\Pi'}\mid\mathcal F_\Pi]=t_\Pi$.
The residual $Z-t_{\Pi'}$ is orthogonal in $L_2$ to every $\mathcal F_{\Pi'}$-measurable square-integrable variable, including $t_{\Pi'}-t_\Pi$. Expanding
$Z-t_\Pi=(Z-t_{\Pi'})+(t_{\Pi'}-t_\Pi)$ proves the identity. The $L_2$ upward martingale convergence theorem gives the limit. Conditional expectation onto $\sigma(\phi(X))$ equals $m(X)$ precisely when the latter is measurable with respect to that sigma-field, since $\E[Z\mid\phi(X)]=\E[m(X)\mid\phi(X)]$.
\end{proof}
Shrinking diameters imply the required generating or differentiation property only for an appropriate differentiation basis. An arbitrary sequence of empirical radii is not sufficient. The same upward-martingale conclusion holds in $L_1$ for integrable $Z$.

\begin{proposition}[Irreducible representation term]\label{prop:representation}
For any square-integrable represented predictor $f(\phi(X))$,
\[
 \E[(m(X)-f(\phi(X)))^2]
 =\E[(m(X)-m_\phi(\phi(X)))^2]
  +\E[(m_\phi(\phi(X))-f(\phi(X)))^2].
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Conditioning on $\phi(X)$ makes the cross term zero because
$\E[m(X)-m_\phi(\phi(X))\mid\phi(X)]=0$.
\end{proof}
For example, if $X$ is uniform on $\{-1,1\}$, $Z=\ind\{X=1\}$ and $\phi$ is constant, the first term is $1/4$. No number of regions in that representation can remove it.

\begin{definition}[Task-specific regional table]
For a hard cell, store $N_A=\sum_i\ind_A(X_i)$,
$S_A=\sum_i\ind_A(X_i)Z_i$, $Q_A=\sum_i\ind_A(X_i)Z_i^2$, geometry and routing. These determine empirical means, variances and binary count intervals. Multiclass output requires the class-count vector. Quantiles require order statistics, an empirical distribution, or a separately controlled quantile sketch. Polynomial correction also requires geometric moments and corrected-score second moments. Causal analysis requires summaries of the relevant scores.
\end{definition}
This table is a reusable computational summary for its declared operations. It is neither a universal sufficient statistic nor a lossless replacement for all observations. In particular, the first two moments alone do not determine quantiles. Spherical geometry costs $O(Kd)$ stored numbers and rank-$r$ geometry $O(Kdr)$; these are geometry costs, not bounds on an exact stored empirical distribution.

\section{Inverse counts, small cells and finite-sample risk}\label{sec:counts}
Fix a construction-measurable cell with probability $p>0$, and let $N\sim\operatorname{Bin}(n,p)$ on an independent estimation sample. Write $\nu=np$.

\begin{lemma}[Exact inverse-count control]\label{lem:inverse}
For all $n\ge1$ and $p>0$,
\[
 \E\frac1{N+1}=\frac{1-(1-p)^{n+1}}{(n+1)p},\qquad
 \E\frac1{N\vee1}\le\frac{2}{(n+1)p}\le\frac2{\nu}.
\]
Moreover $\Pp(N=0)\le e^{-\nu}$ and
$\Pp(N<\nu/2)\le e^{-\nu/8}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use $1/(k+1)=\int_0^1t^k\,dt$ and the binomial generating function:
\[
 \E\frac1{N+1}=\int_0^1(1-p+pt)^n\,dt
 =\frac{1-(1-p)^{n+1}}{(n+1)p}.
\]
For $k=0$, $1/(k\vee1)\le2/(k+1)$; for $k\ge1$ it follows from $k+1\le2k$. Taking expectations proves the second bound. The empty probability is $(1-p)^n\le e^{-np}$. For the lower tail, Markov's inequality applied to $e^{-\lambda N}$ and
$\E e^{-\lambda N}\le\exp\{\nu(e^{-\lambda}-1)\}$ gives the usual multiplicative Chernoff bound
$\Pp\{N\le(1-u)\nu\}\le\exp(-\nu u^2/2)$, $0<u<1$. Substitution $u=1/2$ gives the stated constant.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Simultaneous count event and empty-cell handling]\label{lem:small}
For $M$ frozen candidate cells, possibly overlapping across menus, let
$\pi_{\min}=\min_A P_X(A)>0$. If $n\pi_{\min}\ge8\log(M/\delta)$, then
$N_A\ge n\pi_A/2$ simultaneously with probability at least $1-\delta$.
No independence between different cell counts is required.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse} to each cell and take a union bound:
$\Pp(\exists A:N_A<n\pi_A/2)\le\sum_Ae^{-n\pi_A/8}\le\delta$.
\end{proof}
A zero-count binary region receives $[0,1]$, not a zero-width interval. A bounded mean receives its full allowed range. A minimum count imposed on construction data does not imply the same count or a positive mass bound on estimation data.

\begin{theorem}[Regional concentration and risk with the small-cell remainder]\label{thm:basic}
Conditional on $N_A=k>0$, the $k$ observations in a frozen cell are iid from its conditional law. For $0\le Z\le1$, their mean $\hat t_A$ obeys
\[
 \Pp(|\hat t_A-t_A|>u\mid\mathcal S,N_A=k)\le2e^{-2ku^2}.
\]
With a fallback $a_0\in[0,1]$ when $N_A=0$ and $v_A=\Var(Z\mid A)$,
\[
 \E[(\hat t_A-t_A)^2\mid\mathcal S]
 \le\frac{2v_A}{n\pi_A}+e^{-n\pi_A}.
\]
For an independent query in a partition,
\begin{align}
 \E[(\hat t_\Pi(X^\star)-m(X^\star))^2\mid\mathcal S]
 &\le \frac2n\sum_Av_A
 +\sum_A\pi_A\,\E[(t_A-m(X))^2\mid A]\nonumber\\
 &\quad+\sum_A\pi_Ae^{-n\pi_A}. \label{eq:integrated}
\end{align}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on the membership indicators and their count, the selected pairs have the product conditional law on $A$. Hoeffding's inequality proves concentration. For $k>0$, the mean is unbiased and its MSE is $v_A/k$; on $k=0$ its squared error is at most one. Lemma~\ref{lem:inverse} bounds the expectation. For the query result expand the error around $t_A$. Its cross term vanishes since $\E[t_A-m(X^\star)\mid A]=0$, including on the empty event because the fallback is constant on that cell. Weight the conditional risks by $\pi_A$ and sum.
\end{proof}
If $|m(x)-m(c_A)|\le L_A\rho_A^\alpha$ on $A$, the within-cell variance in \eqref{eq:integrated} is at most $L_A^2\rho_A^{2\alpha}$: the conditional mean $t_A$ minimizes squared error over constants. For a binary outcome, exact binomial test inversion gives Clopper--Pearson coverage for $t_A$ for every $k$, including the full-interval convention at zero.

\section{Geometric correction, its sign and variance}\label{sec:correction}
Let $z=\phi(x)$ and suppose
$m(x)=g(z)+r_\phi(x)$ with $|r_\phi(x)|\le\epsilon_\phi$.
For $q=\lceil\alpha\rceil-1$, assume on the entire represented cell
\[
 \left|g(z)-g(c_A)-\sum_{1\le|u|\le q}D^ug(c_A)(z-c_A)^u/u!\right|
 \le L_A\|z-c_A\|^\alpha.
\]
This Taylor-remainder condition specifies integer smoothness without ambiguity.
A derivative pilot $\hat D_A^u$ is fixed using $\mathcal S$. On a declared pilot event,
$|\hat D_A^u-D^ug(c_A)|\le a_{A,u}$. Set
\[
 P_A(z)=\sum_{1\le|u|\le q}\hat D_A^u(z-c_A)^u/u!,\qquad
 B_A=L_A\rho_A^\alpha+\epsilon_\phi+
       \sum_{1\le|u|\le q}a_{A,u}\rho_A^{|u|}/u!.
\]
Define corrected summands $W_{i,A}=Z_i-P_A(\phi(X_i))$ and $\mu_A=\E[W_{i,A}\mid A,\mathcal S]$.

\begin{theorem}[Corrected centre and reconstructed query]\label{thm:correction}
For $N_A>0$, let $\hat g_A=N_A^{-1}\sum_{i\in A}W_{i,A}$ and
$\hat m_A(x)=\hat g_A+P_A(\phi(x))$. Then
\[
 |\mu_A-g(c_A)|\le B_A,\qquad
 |\E[\hat m_A(x)\mid\mathcal S,N_A]-m(x)|\le2B_A\quad(x\in A).
\]
At first order,
\[
 \hat g_A=\bar Z_A-\hat D_A^\top(\bar z_A-c_A),\qquad
 \hat m_A(x)=\bar Z_A+\hat D_A^\top(\phi(x)-\bar z_A). \label{eq:firstorder}
\]
The conditional sampling variance of either estimate is
$\Var(W_{i,A}\mid A,\mathcal S)/N_A$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Subtract $P_A(z)$ from the Taylor expansion, add $r_\phi(x)$, and bound each monomial by $\rho_A^{|u|}$. This gives a pointwise residual of size $B_A$ around $g(c_A)$, so averaging yields the first inequality. At a query, subtract its own residual from the averaged residual, giving at most $2B_A$. The displayed first-order formula follows by expanding the average polynomial. Adding a fixed query polynomial changes no sampling variance.
\end{proof}
Thus the correction must subtract the empirical geometric moment before adding the query polynomial. Merely adding $\hat D_A^\top(\phi(x)-c_A)$ to $\bar Z_A$ generally leaves first-order bias. A variance computed from raw $Z_i$ also omits variation in the geometric correction.

If $|W_{i,A}|\le H$, Hoeffding applies with range $2H$.
Bernstein applies to $W_{i,A}-\mu_A$, whose absolute bound is $2H$:
\[
 \Pp(|\bar W_A-\mu_A|\ge u\mid N_A=k,\mathcal S)
 \le2\exp\!\left\{-\frac{k u^2}{2v_A+(4H/3)u}\right\}.
\]
Its proof uses the bounded centred summand Bernstein inequality, so a factor for the centred range must not be silently dropped. For $0<\alpha\le1$, $q=0$ is enough; for $\alpha>1$, a piecewise constant estimate does not automatically attain an order-$\alpha$ query rate.

\section{Finite-menu oracle inequalities with complete comparisons}\label{sec:oracle}
Freeze $M$ predictors and all tables needed to evaluate their losses before inspecting $B$ iid validation groups. Their group losses satisfy $L_b(g)\in[0,1]$. Write $R(g)=\E L_b(g)$ and $\hat R(g)=B^{-1}\sum_bL_b(g)$.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-menu and continuous-comparator oracle]\label{thm:oracle}
If $\hat R(\hat g)\le\min_{g\in\mathcal G_\eta}\hat R(g)+\varepsilon_{\rm alg}$, $|\mathcal G_\eta|=M$, then with probability at least $1-\delta$,
\[
 R(\hat g)\le\min_{g\in\mathcal G_\eta}R(g)+2t_B+\varepsilon_{\rm alg},
 \qquad t_B=\sqrt{\log(2M/\delta)/(2B)}.
\]
If every $g$ in a continuous class $\mathcal G$ has a grid representative $g_\eta$ with
$R(g_\eta)\le R(g)+A_\eta$, then the minimum can be replaced by
$\inf_{\mathcal G}R+A_\eta$. If $|R_0(g)-R(g)|\le E$ uniformly on both classes, then
\[
 R_0(\hat g)\le\inf_{\mathcal G}R_0(g)+A_\eta+2t_B+2E+\varepsilon_{\rm alg}. \label{eq:oracle}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each $g$, Hoeffding gives
$\Pp(|\hat R(g)-R(g)|>t)\le2e^{-2Bt^2}$. A union bound gives the event
$\mathcal E=\{\sup_g|\hat R(g)-R(g)|\le t_B\}$ with probability at least $1-\delta$.
On $\mathcal E$, for any grid member $h$,
\[
 R(\hat g)\le\hat R(\hat g)+t_B
 \le\hat R(h)+\varepsilon_{\rm alg}+t_B
 \le R(h)+2t_B+\varepsilon_{\rm alg}.
\]
For a continuous comparator choose $h=g_\eta$, then take an arbitrarily near-optimal $g$ and pass to the infimum. Finally $R_0(\hat g)\le R(\hat g)+E$ and
$R(g)\le R_0(g)+E$, giving the two transfer costs. No sampling error is cancelled across the empirical minimization step.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Nonuniform penalties]\label{thm:penalty}
Let positive weights $w_g$ sum to at most one, and set
$p_g=\sqrt{\log(2/(\delta w_g))/(2B)}$.
If $\hat g$ approximately minimizes $\hat R(g)+p_g$ within $\varepsilon$, then
\[
 R(\hat g)\le\inf_g\{R(g)+2p_g\}+\varepsilon
\]
with probability at least $1-\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Hoeffding at level $\delta w_g$ and a union bound imply $|\hat R(g)-R(g)|\le p_g$ for every $g$. Hence
$R(\hat g)\le\hat R(\hat g)+p_{\hat g}\le\hat R(g)+p_g+\varepsilon\le R(g)+2p_g+\varepsilon$ for every $g$.
\end{proof}
For squared or clipped loss, the declared bound must hold numerically. Unclipped log loss is unbounded; clipping probabilities to $[\zeta,1-\zeta]$ yields loss range at most $-\log\zeta$, which must multiply the deviation term. An oracle inequality for average prediction loss does not by itself imply a pointwise adaptive rate. Section~\ref{sec:adaptive} gives an explicit selector for that different conclusion.

If dependent blocks admit a coupling to $B$ independent blocks that fails with probability at most $\delta_{\rm dep}$, the same proof loses $\delta_{\rm dep}$ in coverage. Alternatively a separately proved additive deviation error $e_{\rm dep}$ is used twice. These are different corrections. Neither is licensed by a guessed effective sample size.

\section{Adaptive multiscale selection}\label{sec:adaptive}
We now make the rate-adaptation statement precise. Let $\mathcal H_n$ be a geometric grid of radii with ratio between two fixed constants, and let $\mathcal D$ be a finite set of local dimensions. A candidate $g=(h,s)$ has cells with
\[
 c_-h^s\le \pi_{g,A}\le c_+h^s,\qquad
 \rho_{g,A}\le C_\rho h,\qquad A\in g.
\]
The constants and the displayed mass law are part of the candidate-family assumption. They are not inferred from an empirical count alone.

\begin{definition}[Adaptive score]
For a bounded score and a finite family $\mathcal G_n$, define
\[
 \operatorname{pen}_n(g)=C_0\sqrt{\frac{\ell_g}{n_V\pi_{\min}(g)}}
       +C_1\frac{\ell_g}{n_E\pi_{\min}(g)},\qquad
 \ell_g=\log\frac{2|\mathcal G_n|K_g}{\delta}.
\]
The validation selector minimizes $\widehat R_V(g)+\operatorname{pen}_n(g)$ over candidates constructed without the validation outcomes. The estimation table is computed on an independent $D_E$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Count-based adaptation to unknown local smoothness and dimension]\label{thm:adaptive}
Condition on independent construction data. For each $x$ in a fixed finite set
$\mathcal X_0$, let $A_h(x)$ be nested positive-mass cells on a finite increasing
geometric grid $\mathcal H$ of $J$ radii. Observations are iid and $0\le Z\le1$.
Write $Q=|\mathcal X_0|$, $\ell=\log(8JQ/\delta)$, $p_h=P_X(A_h(x))$, and assume
\[
 c h^{s_x}\le p_h\le C h^{s_x},\quad n_Ep_h\ge12\ell,
 \quad |t_h-m(x)|\le L_xh^{\alpha_x}+r_x,
 \quad 0<\alpha_x\le1,
\]
where $r_x$ is a supplied nonnegative upper bound. Define
$v_h=\sqrt{\ell/(2(N_h\vee1))}$ and choose the largest radius satisfying
\[
 |\widehat m_h-\widehat m_k|\le2(v_h+v_k+r_x)\quad\text{for all }k\le h.
\]
Suppose $L_x>0$ and the grid contains $h_\star\in[h_0/q,h_0]$, with fixed $q>1$ and
$h_0=\{\ell/(3Cn_EL_x^2)\}^{1/(2\alpha_x+s_x)}$.
Then, with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously over the query set,
\[
 |\widehat m_{\widehat h}(x)-m(x)|
 \le C_{x,q}\{(\ell/n_E)^{\alpha_x/(2\alpha_x+s_x)}+r_x\}.
\]
The selector uses counts and $r_x$, not $\alpha_x$ or $s_x$. Constants can be
uniform over compact positive ranges of $c,C,L_x,s_x,\alpha_x$. A constant target
($L_x=0$) uses the largest admissible grid radius as its oracle scale.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Multiplicative Chernoff bounds give
$P\{N_h\notin[n_Ep_h/2,3n_Ep_h/2]\}\le2e^{-n_Ep_h/12}$.
Their union probability is at most $\delta/4$. Conditional Hoeffding at all
positive counts gives $|\widehat m_h-t_h|\le v_h$ outside a set of probability
at most $2JQe^{-\ell}=\delta/4$. Thus both events hold with probability at least
$1-\delta/2$, and in particular at least $1-\delta$; no zero-count cell occurs
on the count event. On that event,
\[
 \sqrt{\ell/(3Cn_Eh^{s_x})}\le v_h
 \le\sqrt{\ell/(cn_Eh^{s_x})}.
\]
The definition of $h_0$ implies $L_xh_\star^{\alpha_x}\le v_{h_\star}$.
For $k\le h_\star$, nested counts give $v_k\ge v_{h_\star}$, whence
$|\widehat m_{h_\star}-\widehat m_k|
\le L_x(h_\star^{\alpha_x}+k^{\alpha_x})+v_{h_\star}+v_k+2r_x
\le2(v_{h_\star}+v_k+r_x)$.
The oracle scale is admissible, so $\widehat h\ge h_\star$.
Comparing it with $h_\star$ gives
$|\widehat m_{\widehat h}-m(x)|\le6v_{h_\star}+3r_x$.
The upper bound on $v_{h_\star}$ and $h_\star\ge h_0/q$ give the stated
rate. The same argument uses $s_x,\alpha_x$ only for analysis, not for selection.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous local-dimension adaptation and family selection]\label{prop:dimadapt}
Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive} holds when the local exponent $s_x$ varies with $x$.
For $F$ externally constructed hierarchies, it holds simultaneously for each
hierarchy after replacing $JQ$ by the total number of hierarchy--query--scale
triples. This does not establish a pointwise minimum over unrelated hierarchies.
If, in addition, supplied radii $B_f(x)$ obey
$|\widehat m_f(x)-m(x)|\le B_f(x)$ simultaneously, the implementable rule
$\widehat f=\arg\min_f B_f(x)$ obeys
$|\widehat m_{\widehat f}(x)-m(x)|\le\min_fB_f(x)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply the count and Hoeffding union bounds to the enlarged finite index set;
the proof at each query uses its own mass exponent. The last inequality is
immediate on the simultaneous event. A comparison involving an uncontrolled
$v_{\widehat f}$ cannot be replaced by the comparator radius; this is why the
additional supplied radii are needed for selection across unrelated families.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Minimax benchmark and the exact scope of adaptation]\label{thm:minimax}
Fix $s\le d$ and $\alpha\in(0,1]$. Let $X\sim\operatorname{Unif}([0,1]^s)$ (or have a design density bounded above and below by positive constants near the interior query point), and consider Bernoulli regression functions in a Hölder ball of radius $L$ bounded between $1/4$ and $3/4$. For any estimator based on $n$ iid observations,
\[
 \inf_{\widehat m}\sup_{m}\E_m\{\widehat m(x_0)-m(x_0)\}^2
 \ge c\,n^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha+s)}
\]
at an interior point $x_0$. The estimator in Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive} attains this order up to logarithmic menu factors when representation, geometry and optimization remainders are $o(n^{-\alpha/(2\alpha+s)})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $h=n^{-1/(2\alpha+s)}$. Place a bump
$\psi_h(x)=h^\alpha\psi((x-x_0)/h)$ supported in a cube of side $h$, where $\psi$ is a fixed $C^\alpha$ function with $\psi(0)=1$. Let $m_0\equiv1/2$ and $m_1=1/2+\lambda\psi_h$ with $\lambda$ small enough for the Hölder ball and probability bounds. Their pointwise separation is $\lambda h^\alpha$. For Bernoulli observations, the Kullback--Leibler divergence is bounded by
$C n\int(m_1-m_0)^2dx\le C'n h^{2\alpha+s}$. The selected $h$ makes this bounded by a constant. Le Cam's two-point inequality then gives a constant lower bound on the probability of pointwise error at least $\lambda h^\alpha/2$, which implies the displayed squared-risk lower bound. For the upper bound, use Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive} with $\delta=n^{-2}$ and clip the estimate to $[0,1]$. Its squared risk is bounded by the squared high-probability bound plus $n^{-2}$; the logarithmic factor includes this failure budget. The grid and count conditions must hold at that budget. This is an adaptation benchmark for the declared finite-dimensional Hölder family; it is not a claim about unrestricted learned encoders or arbitrary boundary distributions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What “adaptive optimal” means here]
The theorem establishes adaptation over a finite smoothness--dimension range and a verified scale/mass menu. The supplied remainder bound $r_x$ is part of the selector contract; if it is estimated rather than known, its upper-confidence event must be added to the probability budget. It does not assert adaptation over an unrestricted encoder, arbitrary unknown support, or all hard partition trees. Those classes require additional entropy and approximation assumptions. This distinction is part of the theorem, not a limitation hidden outside it.
\end{remark}

\section{Encoder representation error and complexity}\label{sec:encoder}
Let $\Phi$ be a class of measurable encoders. For each $\phi\in\Phi$ define the represented target $m_\phi(z)=\E[Z\mid\phi(X)=z]$. A loss-covering metric $d_\Phi$ is assumed to satisfy
\[
 |m_\phi(\phi(x))-m_{\phi'}(\phi'(x))|
 \le L_\Phi d_\Phi(\phi,\phi')+\epsilon_{\Phi,\rm id}(x)
\]
for the declared target class. The term $\epsilon_{\Phi,\rm id}$ captures information lost by the representation relative to the target and cannot be inferred from parameter count.

\begin{proposition}[Product cover for encoders, shapes and scales]\label{prop:cover}
Let $\Phi,\mathcal S,\mathcal H,\mathcal D,\Theta$ have covers with cardinalities
$N_\Phi(\varepsilon_\Phi)$, $N_\mathcal S(\varepsilon_\mathcal S)$,
$N_\mathcal H(\varepsilon_\mathcal H)$, $N_\mathcal D(\varepsilon_\mathcal D)$ and
$N_\Theta(\varepsilon_\Theta)$. The product family has cover size
\[
 M(\varepsilon)=N_\Phi N_\mathcal S N_\mathcal H N_\mathcal D N_\Theta,
\quad
 \log M=\sum_u\log N_u.
\]
If validation loss and geometric envelope are respectively $L_u$-Lipschitz in factor $u$, the grid approximation is
\[
 A_\varepsilon\le\sum_u L_u\varepsilon_u
\]
and the validation penalty is
\[
 2\sqrt{\frac{\sum_u\log N_u+\log(2/\delta)}{2B}}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Choose the nearest cover element separately for every factor. The Cartesian product has the product cardinality and hence the sum of logarithms. Apply the Lipschitz inequalities to the loss and envelope and then Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle} to the finite product cover.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-resolution loss chaining]\label{thm:entropyintegral}
Let $L_g\in[0,1]$ be a separable family of frozen group-loss functions, with
$B$ iid groups. Let $\mathcal E_j$ be a finite cover in the uniform loss metric
$d(g,h)=\sup_z|L_g(z)-L_h(z)|$ of radius $\epsilon_j=2^{-j}$ and cardinality $N_j$.
Define
\begin{align*}
 D_J={}&\sqrt{\frac{\log\{2(J+1)N_0/\delta\}}{2B}}+2\epsilon_J\\
 &+\sum_{j=1}^J3\epsilon_j
 \sqrt{\frac{2\log\{2(J+1)N_jN_{j-1}/\delta\}}{B}}.
\end{align*}
Then $\sup_g|(P_B-P)L_g|\le D_J$ with probability at least $1-\delta$.
An $\varepsilon_{\rm alg}$ approximate empirical minimizer on $\mathcal E_J$
satisfies
$R(\widehat g)\le\inf_gR(g)+\epsilon_J+2D_J+\varepsilon_{\rm alg}$.
For factor covers in metrics with uniform loss moduli $L_u$, choose factor
meshes $\eta_{u,j}$ such that $\sum_uL_u\eta_{u,j}\le\epsilon_j$; then
$\log N_j\le\sum_u\log N_u(\eta_{u,j})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Choose $g_j\in\mathcal E_j$ with $d(g,g_j)\le\epsilon_j$.
The identity is
$L_g=L_{g_0}+\sum_{j=1}^J(L_{g_j}-L_{g_{j-1}})+(L_g-L_{g_J})$.
The last centered term is at most $2\epsilon_J$.
The base functions have range one; Hoeffding and a union bound over $N_0$
functions give the first term with error $\delta/(J+1)$.
For each realizable adjacent pair,
$|L_{g_j}-L_{g_{j-1}}|\le\epsilon_j+\epsilon_{j-1}=3\epsilon_j$.
The range length is at most $6\epsilon_j$; Hoeffding over at most
$N_jN_{j-1}$ pairs gives the displayed increment radius, each with error
$\delta/(J+1)$. Summing these $J+1$ budgets proves the uniform bound.
Apply it twice in the empirical minimization comparison and use the finest
cover for an arbitrarily near-optimal population candidate. Product covers give
the final entropy statement. No parameter metric is a loss metric for hard
routing unless a separate transfer argument establishes that fact.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Width-correct neural encoder cover]\label{thm:neuralcover}
Let a ReLU network have widths $d_0,\ldots,d_L$, $P=\sum_{\ell=1}^L(d_\ell d_{\ell-1}+d_\ell)$
scalar parameters in $[-R_\theta,R_\theta]$, input norm at most $R_X$, and
$\|W_\ell\|_{\rm op}\le S_\ell$, $\|b_\ell\|_2\le B_\ell$.
Set $A_0=R_X$, $A_\ell=S_\ell A_{\ell-1}+B_\ell$ and
\[
 C_{\rm enc}=\sum_{\ell=1}^L
 \{\sqrt{d_\ell d_{\ell-1}}A_{\ell-1}+\sqrt{d_\ell}\}
 \prod_{j=\ell+1}^LS_j.
\]
For a loss $L_{\rm out}$-Lipschitz in the encoder output,
\[
 \log N_\Phi(\varepsilon)
 \le P\log\left(2+\frac{2R_\theta L_{\rm out}C_{\rm enc}}{\varepsilon}\right).
\]
If $L_{\rm out}C_{\rm enc}=0$, one representative suffices.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Divide the parameter box into cubes of side $\eta$ and choose one feasible
parameter from each occupied cube. There are at most
$(2+2R_\theta/\eta)^P$ cubes; this preserves the operator and bias constraints.
Two parameters in one cube satisfy
$\|W_\ell-W'_\ell\|_{\rm op}\le\|W_\ell-W'_\ell\|_F
\le\sqrt{d_\ell d_{\ell-1}}\eta$ and
$\|b_\ell-b'_\ell\|_2\le\sqrt{d_\ell}\eta$.
If $D_\ell$ is their worst output difference at layer $\ell$, the ReLU
contraction and these inequalities give
$D_\ell\le S_\ell D_{\ell-1}
\eta(\sqrt{d_\ell d_{\ell-1}}A_{\ell-1}+\sqrt{d_\ell})$.
Iteration gives $D_L\le C_{\rm enc}\eta$. Set
$\eta=\varepsilon/(L_{\rm out}C_{\rm enc})$ and take logarithms.
\end{proof}
The theorem is a complexity statement for a fixed bounded architecture. It does not claim that an arbitrary trained encoder has a finite usable entropy term. A learned encoder may instead be handled by an independent structure split, a data-dependent stability bound, or a public codebook.

\begin{theorem}[Constructive cover for diagonal hard shapes]\label{thm:diagcover}
Assume $\|x\|_2\le R$, $W\in[-w,w]^{r\times d}$, $c\in[-C,C]^r$,
$a\in[a_-,a_+]^r$ with $a_->0$, and $h\in[h_-,h_+]$ with $h_->0$. For
\[
 q_{W,c,a,h}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^r a_j\{(Wx)_j-c_j\}^2-h^2,
 \qquad A_{W,c,a,h}=\{x:q_{W,c,a,h}(x)\le0\},
\]
endpoint-inclusive Cartesian grids with mesh $(t_W,t_c,t_a,t_h)$ have cover sizes
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \log N_W&\le rd\log\!\left(2+\frac{2w\sqrt{rd}}{t_W}\right),&
 \log N_c&\le r\log\!\left(2+\frac{2C\sqrt r}{t_c}\right),\\
 \log N_a&\le r\log\!\left(2+\frac{a_+-a_-}{t_a}\right),&
 \log N_h&\le \log\!\left(2+\frac{h_+-h_-}{t_h}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
Writing $Z_0=w\sqrt{rd}\,R+C\sqrt r$, the induced score error is bounded by
\[
 \sup_{\|x\|\le R}|q_{W,c,a,h}(x)-q_{W',c',a',h'}(x)|
 \le2a_+Z_0(Rt_W+t_c)+Z_0^2t_a+2h_+t_h.
\]
The dimension index $r$ is discrete; its entropy is the logarithm of the sum of these cover sizes over the declared finite range of $r$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A coordinate grid in a $p$-dimensional box with mesh $t/\sqrt p$ has at most
$(2+L\sqrt p/t)^p$ points and Euclidean rounding error at most $t$; applying this to each parameter block gives the four displayed counts. Put $z=Wx-c$ and $z'=W'x-c'$. Then $\|z\|,\|z'\|\le Z_0$ and $\|z-z'\|\le Rt_W+t_c$. The identity
\[
 |z^\top\!\operatorname{diag}(a)z-z'^\top\!\operatorname{diag}(a')z'|
 \le2a_+Z_0\|z-z'\|+Z_0^2\|a-a'\|_\infty
\]
and $|h^2-h'^2|\le2h_+|h-h'|$ prove the score bound. A finite union over $r$ gives the logarithm of the sum of the cover sizes; it is at most their largest log size plus the logarithm of the number of allowed dimensions.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Encoder representation decomposition]\label{thm:encdecomp}
Suppose $m(x)=g(\phi(x))+r_\phi(x)$, $|r_\phi(x)|\le\epsilon_\phi$, and
$|g(z)-g(z')|\le L_g\|z-z'\|^\alpha$ for $0<\alpha\le1$.
The centre $c_A$ lies in representation space. If the support radius is
$\rho_A$, then, for $P_X$-almost every $x\in A$,
\[
 |m(x)-g(c_A)|\le\epsilon_\phi+L_g\rho_A^\alpha,
 \qquad |m(x)-t_A|\le2(\epsilon_\phi+L_g\rho_A^\alpha).
\]
For a specified fixed query use its actual distance to $c_A$ in the first term.
A raw-input quantity $m(c_A)$ is undefined unless a raw representative is
specified; if $x_A\in A$ is such a representative, the same regional bound
implies $|m(x)-m(x_A)|\le2\epsilon_\phi+2L_g\rho_A^\alpha$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The first inequality follows by subtracting $g(c_A)$ in the representation
formula. Average it over $A$ to bound $|t_A-g(c_A)|$ and apply the triangle
inequality. Apply the first inequality twice for $x,x_A$. No inverse encoder
is assumed.\end{proof}

\section{Hard boundaries, shape entropy and the continuous family}\label{sec:shapes}
A parameter cover controls a hard allocation only if small parameter changes cause small probability changes. We make the needed boundary condition explicit.

Let a nominal cell be $A_\theta=\{x:q_\theta(x)\le0\}$, where $\theta$ collects centre, radius, shape and encoder parameters. Suppose
\[
 \sup_x|q_\theta(x)-q_{\theta'}(x)|\le\eta,\qquad
 P_X\{|q_\theta(X)|\le t\}\le C_\partial t^\kappa.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Hard-cell boundary transfer]\label{lem:boundary}
Under these conditions,
\[
 P_X(A_\theta\triangle A_{\theta'})\le C_\partial\eta^\kappa.
\]
If $P_X(A_\theta),P_X(A_{\theta'})\ge\pi_0$ and $0\le Z\le1$, then
\[
 |\E[Z\mid A_\theta]-\E[Z\mid A_{\theta'}]|
 \le\frac{2C_\partial\eta^\kappa}{\pi_0}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Membership can change only when $q_\theta$ lies within $\eta$ of zero. The symmetric difference is therefore contained in $\{|q_\theta|\le\eta\}$. For the conditional mean, numerator and denominator each change by at most the symmetric-difference probability. Applying the ratio identity
$u/v-u'/v'=(u-u')/v'+u(v'-v)/(vv')$ and $u\le v$ gives the factor $2/\pi_0$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Allocation-parameter transfer and query perturbations are distinct.}
Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} concerns the probability of membership changes when the allocation parameters change. For a frozen nearest-centre map in a common declared norm, let $d_{(1)}(z)\le d_{(2)}(z)$ be the two smallest centre distances. Every centre-distance function is 1-Lipschitz in that norm; hence $b_{\rm norm}(z)=(d_{(2)}(z)-d_{(1)}(z))/2$ is a conservative query-level preservation radius. Indeed, after a perturbation $\delta$, every competing gap remains at least $d_{(2)}-d_{(1)}-2\|\delta\|>0$ when $\|\delta\|<b_{\rm norm}$. Ties have zero margin. This statement is separate from empirical radial coverage, does not establish probability accuracy, and assumes that centres, metric and tie rule remain fixed. For a positive-definite common Mahalanobis metric the exact bisector formula below applies in transformed coordinates; for distinct per-cell metrics an actual score-gap perturbation bound is required. A child split adds another boundary: its hyperplane normal and the root boundary must be evaluated in the same declared perturbation coordinates.


\begin{definition}[Region-preserving perturbation and certificate margins]\label{def:perturbation_margin}
Fix a frozen allocation, metric and tie rule.  For a represented query $z$ assigned to
cell $j$, let $d_{(1)}(z)\le d_{(2)}(z)$ be the two smallest centre distances and
let $d_j(z)=\norm{z-c_j}$.  The geometric assignment and envelope margins are
\[
 m_{\Pi}(z)=\frac{d_{(2)}(z)-d_{(1)}(z)}{2},\qquad
 m_{B}(z)=\rho_j-d_j(z).
\]
A declared perturbation budget $\tau$ is measured in the same representation norm;
more generally, it is required to satisfy
$\norm{\phi(x+\delta)-\phi(x)}\le\tau$ for every accepted query.  Write $D^\delta$ for
the joint law of the perturbed query and its outcome under the declared noise
mechanism; the response-drift term below compares its conditional law with that
of $D$.  A certified cell-level margin is any lower bound
\[
 \underline m_j\le \mathop{\rm ess\,inf}_{X\in A_j}
       \min\{m_{\Pi}(\phi(X)),m_B(\phi(X))\}
\]
obtained on the independent assignment-audit role.  The empirical minimum or a
quantile without a simultaneous lower-confidence argument is a diagnostic, not a
certificate.  The robust non-inferiority slack at budget $\tau$ is defined by
\[
 s_j(\tau)=-U_j-
 \frac{L_{\ell}}{4}(\lambda_j+\lambda_{\rm pt})\tau
 -2\Delta_Y(\tau)-d^{(\pm)}_{{\rm bdry},j}(\tau),
\]
where $U_j$ is the clean paired-loss upper bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate_sufficiency},
$\lambda_j$ and $\lambda_{\rm pt}$ are declared logit Lipschitz bounds,
$L_{\ell}$ is a loss Lipschitz constant in the probability argument, and
$\Delta_Y(\tau)$ is an optional conditional-response drift budget.  The boundary
term is zero for a certified assignment-preserving perturbation and otherwise is
computed from the symmetric-difference bound in Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Robust non-inferiority inside the certificate margin]\label{thm:robust_margin}
Let $Q_j$ be an accepted regional Logistic head and $q_{\rm pt}$ the frozen point
Logistic head on cell $A_j$, and let $R_D$ use a bounded loss that is
$L_{\ell}$-Lipschitz in its probability argument (Brier loss or clipped
log loss are examples).  Assume that, for all accepted perturbations with
$\norm{\phi(x+\delta)-\phi(x)}\le\tau$, (i) the response conditional law is
unchanged up to total variation $\Delta_Y(\tau)$, (ii) the two logits are
$\lambda_j$- and $\lambda_{\rm pt}$-Lipschitz in the declared representation
norm, and (iii) the perturbation is assignment-preserving whenever
$\tau<\underline m_j$.  If the clean paired-loss certificate gives $U_j\le0$,
then for every $\tau<\underline m_j$,
\[
 R_{D^{\delta}}(Q_j)-R_{D^{\delta}}(q_{\rm pt})
 \le U_j+\frac{L_{\ell}}{4}(\lambda_j+\lambda_{\rm pt})\tau
       +2\Delta_Y(\tau).
\]
Consequently, if $s_j(\tau)\ge0$ with the boundary term set to zero, the regional
head remains certified non-inferior to point Logistic at that perturbation budget.
For Brier loss one may take $L_{\ell}=2$, giving the sensitivity term
$(\lambda_j+\lambda_{\rm pt})\tau/2$.  If the response law is invariant,
$\Delta_Y(\tau)=0$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The logistic sigmoid is $1/4$-Lipschitz, so the change in each prediction is at
most $\lambda\tau/4$ while the assignment is unchanged.  The loss Lipschitz
condition transfers these prediction changes to the two risks.  A conditional
response-law change bounded in total variation changes each $[0,1]$ risk by at
most $\Delta_Y(\tau)$, hence contributes $2\Delta_Y(\tau)$ to their difference.
The clean paired-loss bound $U_j$ and the triangle inequality give the display.
The Brier specialization follows from $|\partial(q-y)^2/\partial q|\le2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Boundary crossing, re-certification and fail-closed routing]\label{prop:boundary_failclosed}
Under the boundary-mass condition of Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary}, a perturbation of
size $\tau$ has cell-disagreement probability
\[
 \beta_j(\tau)=P_D(A_j\triangle A_j^\delta)\le C_{\partial,j}\tau^{\kappa_j}.
\]
If $C_{\partial,j}\tau^{\kappa_j}<\underline\pi_j$, the signed paired-loss transfer
term required when the regional table is reused after that perturbation is at most
\[
 d^{(\pm)}_{{\rm bdry},j}(\tau)
 \le \frac{4C_{\partial,j}\tau^{\kappa_j}}
 {\underline\pi_j-C_{\partial,j}\tau^{\kappa_j}}.
\]
The released contract does not silently reuse a table when $\tau\ge\underline m_j$,
when an observed assignment-change rate exceeds its declared budget, or when the
resulting robust slack is negative.  It instead (i) regenerates assignments,
support, boundaries, probability heads and action fingerprints and re-runs the
certificate, or (ii) invokes the point Logistic fallback, or (iii) abstains.
If a deployment protocol deliberately keeps the old regional table, it must include
$d^{(\pm)}_{{\rm bdry},j}(\tau)$ in the certificate and report the affected mass;
no regional accuracy or robustness gain is implied merely by a small average
assignment-change rate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The symmetric-difference bound follows by applying the score perturbation condition
in Lemma~\ref{lem:boundary} with $\eta=\tau$ (or with the declared score-gap
modulus).  Substitute $b_j=\beta_j(\tau)$ and the target support lower bound into
the signed-loss ratio bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:certificate_sufficiency}.  The
three routing outcomes are the contract definition; they preserve validity because
reuse is permitted only on the original certified event, while re-certification
creates a new event and point fallback makes no regional claim.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation for the noise experiment.}
The perturbation audit therefore has three predeclared regimes.  Clean and
moderate perturbations must satisfy $\tau<\underline m_j$ and $s_j(\tau)\ge0$;
only there can a lower sensitivity envelope for GB--Logistic--S be compared with
point Logistic.  Near-margin perturbations are a re-certification test and must
report assignment-change rate, affected mass and certificate status.  Perturbations
that cross the margin, change support or violate the response-drift budget are
fail-closed cases: the reported route is point Logistic or abstention, not a
regional robustness claim.  Thus a noise result can support the intended statement
only when clean risk is non-inferior, the certified intermediate regime has a
smaller observed or bounded degradation slope, and large or boundary-crossing
perturbations trigger timely fallback.

\begin{theorem}[Continuous hard-shape oracle with a loss transfer]\label{thm:hardshape}
Suppose $K$ ordered envelopes with a fixed first-eligible allocation and a
complement cell have uniform boundary mass $C_\partial t^\kappa$ and a score
cover of error $\eta$. Predictors and their tables are frozen before validation.
Include the table values in the cover: on unchanged routes let the prediction
perturbation be at most $b_{\rm tab}$. Suppose $0\le L\le1$ and the loss is
$L_y$-Lipschitz in its prediction. For iid group validation assume the boundary
condition under the group-balanced row law. Then a grid of size $M$ and an
$\varepsilon_{\rm alg}$ approximate grid minimizer obey
\[
 R(\widehat g)\le\inf_gR(g)+K C_\partial\eta^\kappa
 +L_y b_{\rm tab}+2\sqrt{\log(2M/\delta)/(2B)}+\varepsilon_{\rm alg}
\]
with probability at least $1-\delta$. For tables equal to population cell means,
one may use $b_{\rm tab}\le2K C_\partial\eta^\kappa/\pi_0$ if all allocation
cell masses are at least $\pi_0$. Empirically refitted tables require their own
transfer or a separate table-value cover.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
A changed allocation requires a membership change in at least one envelope,
so its probability is at most $K C_\partial\eta^\kappa$. Its loss contribution
is at most this probability. On unchanged routes the loss change is at most
$L_y b_{\rm tab}$. For population tables apply the numerator/denominator
ratio bound to the allocation cells, each of whose symmetric differences is
contained in the allocation-disagreement event. These bounds give the population
cover error. Theorem~\ref{thm:oracle} supplies the finite-grid comparison.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Generated-tree optimization remainder]\label{prop:greedy}
Let $\mathcal F_{\rm path}\subseteq\mathcal F_{\rm pool}\subseteq\mathcal F_{\rm cont}$ be the
generated path, the audited finite split pool and the continuous comparator, all evaluated by
the same objective $J$. Let $\widehat T\in\mathcal F_{\rm path}$, let
$\varepsilon_{\rm path}=J(\widehat T)-\min_{T\in\mathcal F_{\rm path}}J(T)$, and let
$L_{\rm pool}\le\min_{T\in\mathcal F_{\rm pool}}J(T)$ be a certified lower bound. If
$\varepsilon_{\rm mesh}=\min_{\mathcal F_{\rm pool}}J-\inf_{\mathcal F_{\rm cont}}J$, then
\[
 J(\widehat T)-\inf_{T\in\mathcal F_{\rm cont}}J(T)
 \le \varepsilon_{\rm path}
 +\{\min_{T\in\mathcal F_{\rm path}}J(T)-L_{\rm pool}\}
 +\varepsilon_{\rm mesh}.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Insert the two intermediate quantities $\min_{T\in\mathcal F_{\rm path}}J(T)$ and
$\min_{T\in\mathcal F_{\rm pool}}J(T)$ and telescope. The first difference is
$\varepsilon_{\rm path}$. Since $L_{\rm pool}$ is a lower bound, the second difference is at
most $\min_{T\in\mathcal F_{\rm path}}J(T)-L_{\rm pool}$. The final difference is
$\varepsilon_{\rm mesh}$ by definition. No local greedy gain is identified with a global
tree optimum unless the second term is separately bounded.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Parameter discontinuity does not preclude VC control]\label{prop:atom}
Let $P(X=-1)=P(X=0)=1/2$, $Z=\ind\{X=0\}$ and
$A_\theta=\{x:x-\theta\le0\}$. For $-1<\theta<0$, the conditional mean on
$A_\theta$ is zero, whereas on $A_0$ it is $1/2$. Parameter closeness therefore
does not imply a vanishing hard-cell modulus. Finite-VC indicator classes can
nevertheless have distribution-free uniform concentration. An entirely
unrestricted family of measurable sets has no such finite complexity bound.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Directly evaluate the two masses and numerators. Threshold sets have VC dimension
one, illustrating the distinction from a parameter-continuity theorem. For the
last claim, under a nonatomic design the unrestricted family contains the
sample's finite set of observed covariates, whose empirical mass is one and
population mass zero. Its supremum deviation cannot vanish.\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Boundary-free soft membership cover]\label{prop:softcover}
For $w_\theta(x)=\sigma(-q_\theta(x)/\tau)$ with $\tau\ge\tau_0>0$, if
$\sup_x|q_\theta(x)-q_{\theta'}(x)|\le\eta$ and both denominators are at least $\pi_0$, then for $0\le Z\le1$,
\[
 \left|\frac{\E[w_\theta Z]}{\E[w_\theta]}-\frac{\E[w_{\theta'}Z]}{\E[w_{\theta'}]}\right|
 \le\frac{\eta}{2\tau_0\pi_0}.
\]
For a normalized $K$-region allocation with denominator at least $s_0$, the coordinatewise bound is multiplied by at most $(K+1)/s_0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The logistic derivative is at most $1/4$, hence $\|w_\theta-w_{\theta'}\|_\infty\le\eta/(4\tau_0)$. The numerator and denominator of the ratio each change by at most this quantity. Applying the ratio identity and using $0\le\E[wZ]\le\E[w]$ gives the factor $2/\pi_0$, which is the displayed bound. Normalization changes one denominator and $K$ numerators; summing the same ratio bounds gives the stated factor.
\end{proof}

\section{Independent-refit central limit theory}\label{sec:clt}
We first state the iid cell result, then the group-level ratio result used for EEG and batch data.

\begin{theorem}[Studentized iid regional CLT]\label{thm:iidclt}
Conditional on a fixed construction object, consider independent refit samples of increasing size, and let $W_{i,A}$ be iid in its fixed cell with mean $\mu_A$, variance $\sigma_A^2\in(0,\infty)$ and $\E|W_{i,A}-\mu_A|^{2+\eta}\le C$ for some $\eta>0$. If $N_A\to\infty$ in probability and the sample variance $\hat\sigma_A^2$ is computed from the same independent refit observations, then
\[
 \frac{\sqrt{N_A}(\bar W_A-\mu_A)}{\hat\sigma_A}\Rightarrow N(0,1),\qquad
 \hat\sigma_A^2\to_p\sigma_A^2.
\]
If $\sqrt{N_A}|\mu_A-m(c_A)|\to0$, the same limit is centred at $m(c_A)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Conditional on construction, the Lindeberg condition follows from the uniform $(2+\eta)$ moment bound and $N_A\to\infty$. The Lindeberg--Feller theorem gives the numerator CLT. The conditional law of large numbers gives consistency of the first two sample moments and hence $\hat\sigma_A^2\to_p\sigma_A^2$. Slutsky's theorem gives studentization. The final centering replacement follows from the displayed undersmoothing condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Group-balanced regional target]\label{def:group}
For iid groups $G=1,\ldots,B$, let $U_g=Q_g^{-1}\sum_iw_g(X_{gi})Z_{gi}$ and
$V_g=Q_g^{-1}\sum_iw_g(X_{gi})$, with $0\le w_g\le1$ and $EV_g>0$. The target is
$\theta=EU_g/EV_g$. This is a uniform-group target. It differs from a row-weighted target if group sizes are informative.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Group ratio CLT and variance consistency]\label{thm:ratioCLT}
Assume iid groups, arbitrary within-group dependence, $\E(U_g^2+V_g^2)<\infty$, $EV_g=\nu>0$, and
$\Var(U_g-\theta V_g)>0$. Let $\hat\theta=\bar U/\bar V$ and
\[
 \hat\xi_g=\frac{U_g-\hat\theta V_g}{\bar V},\qquad
 \hat\sigma^2=B^{-1}\sum_g(\hat\xi_g-\bar{\hat\xi})^2.
\]
Then
\[
 \frac{\sqrt B(\hat\theta-\theta)}{\hat\sigma}\Rightarrow N(0,1),\qquad
 \hat\sigma^2\to_p\frac{\Var(U_g-\theta V_g)}{\nu^2}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The vector $\bar T=(\bar U,\bar V)$ satisfies the bivariate CLT. The map $f(u,v)=u/v$ is differentiable at $(EU,EV)$ with gradient $(1/\nu,-\theta/\nu)$. The delta method gives
$\sqrt B(\hat\theta-\theta)=B^{-1/2}\sum_g(U_g-\theta V_g)/\nu+o_p(1)$.
The law of large numbers gives $\bar V\to_p\nu$ and the sample second moment of $\hat\xi_g$ converges to the variance of $(U-\theta V)/\nu$. Studentization and Slutsky prove the result. The formula remains valid under arbitrary within-group dependence because only group independence is used.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Independent refit after selection]\label{cor:refit}
Condition on a selected structure and let the independent refit size increase.
For a fixed selected object, Theorem~\ref{thm:ratioCLT} applies if its displayed
moment, mass and variance conditions hold. For a sequence of selected objects,
one additionally needs a conditional triangular-array CLT and variance
consistency uniformly on construction events whose probabilities tend to one;
independence alone is insufficient. Theorem~\ref{thm:iidclt} supplies one explicit
iid-cell version. A shrinking group-ratio denominator requires its own scaled
Lindeberg and denominator-consistency conditions.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed object apply the group CLT to iid independent refit groups. For a
changing object, integrate its conditional bounded-test-function convergence
when that convergence holds in probability and is bounded; this gives
unconditional convergence. Finite-sample failure probabilities can be added by
Proposition~\ref{prop:tower}; a CLT is an asymptotic limit, not a finite-sample
coverage event.\end{proof}

\section{Selection on the same sample}\label{sec:selective}
There are two valid routes after outcome-dependent selection. Independent refitting gives ordinary conditional inference. Same-sample selection requires either simultaneous inference over the complete finite menu or a selective law for the selection event.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-menu simultaneous refit inference]\label{thm:simul}
Let $\mathcal M$ contain $M$ frozen candidate cells and let $D_E$ be used both to compute all regional estimates and to choose an index $\hat g$ by any measurable rule. For each candidate construct a $(1-\delta/M)$ valid interval $I_g$ for its regional mean or binomial probability. Then
\[
 \mathbb P\{\theta_{\hat g}\in I_{\hat g}\}\ge1-\delta.
\]
For a bounded score, Hoeffding intervals with radius
$\sqrt{\log(2M/\delta)/(2N_g)}$ on the count event are valid; exact binomial inversion is preferred for binary responses.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The union bound gives $\mathbb P\{\forall g:\theta_g\in I_g\}\ge1-\delta$. On this event the selected index is one of the covered indices, regardless of how it was selected. No independence between the selector and the estimation outcomes is needed. The price is the menu multiplicity and potentially wide intervals.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Stable same-sample selection CLT]\label{thm:stableselect}
Let $g^\star$ be the unique minimizer of a population risk over a finite menu, with gap
$\Delta=\min_{g\ne g^\star}\{R(g)-R(g^\star)\}>0$. Let $\hat g$ be an empirical risk minimizer, or an approximate minimizer with objective gap $o_p(\Delta)$. Suppose the empirical risks are uniformly consistent at rate $o_p(\Delta)$, and each candidate estimator has an asymptotically linear representation with a joint multivariate CLT. Then
\[
 \mathbb P(\hat g=g^\star)\to1
\]
and the selected estimator has the same limiting normal law as the estimator indexed by $g^\star$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Uniform consistency implies that, with probability tending to one, every empirical risk difference has the same sign as its population difference, so $\hat g=g^\star$. For any bounded continuous test function $f$, the difference between $f(\widehat\theta_{\hat g})$ and $f(\widehat\theta_{g^\star})$ vanishes in probability on the selection event. The joint CLT for the finite vector and Slutsky's theorem give the selected limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Why naive same-sample Gaussian selection is false at ties]\label{prop:winner}
Let $\hat\theta_1,\hat\theta_2$ be independent $N(0,1/n)$ estimators and select $\hat g=\arg\max_g\hat\theta_g$. Then
$\sqrt n\hat\theta_{\hat g}=\max(Z_1,Z_2)$, which is not normal. Thus a claim of an unconditional naive CLT after arbitrary same-sample selection is false even for a two-element finite menu.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The distribution function is
$\mathbb P\{\max(Z_1,Z_2)\le t\}=\Phi(t)^2$, not $\Phi(t)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Gaussian polyhedral pivot and the scope of approximation]\label{thm:polyselect}
Let $Y\sim N(\mu,\Sigma)$ with known positive definite $\Sigma$, a fixed
nonzero contrast $a$, and an observable selection event $E=\{AY\le b\}$.
Set $v=a^T\Sigma a$, $c=\Sigma a/v$, $T=a^TY$ and $U=Y-cT$.
For each row put $d_i=(Ac)_i$ and $r_i(U)=b_i-(AU)_i$, and define
\[
 L(U)=\max_{d_i<0}r_i(U)/d_i,\qquad
 H(U)=\min_{d_i>0}r_i(U)/d_i.
\]
Empty extrema are $-\infty,+\infty$; rows with $d_i=0$ require $r_i(U)\ge0$.
Conditionally on $U$ and $E$, $T$ has the $N(a^T\mu,v)$ law truncated to
$[L(U),H(U)]$. Its truncated-normal CDF evaluated at $T$ is uniform; inversion
in $a^T\mu$ gives exact conditional confidence intervals.
For a sequence of experiments whose joint laws are within $\epsilon_n\to0$
in total variation of such Gaussian laws, assume both the actual and Gaussian
selection probabilities satisfy $P_n(E_n),P_n^{G}(E_n)\ge p_0>0$. Then the same Gaussian pivot
has coverage $1-\delta+o(1)$ conditional on $E_n$, averaged
over the nuisance projection. Uniform validity at every fixed value of $U_n$
requires an additional conditional approximation and does not follow from
joint total variation alone.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\operatorname{Cov}(U,T)=\Sigma a-cv=0$, so Gaussian independence holds.
Given $U$, each polyhedral constraint is $d_iT\le r_i(U)$, which gives the
stated interval. Conditional Gaussian truncation and the probability integral
transform give the uniform pivot and its inversion. For any two laws with
TV distance $\epsilon_n$, restriction and normalization to an event of
probability at least $p_0$ changes conditional probabilities by at most
$2\epsilon_n/p_0$. Apply this to the measurable pivot-acceptance event.
Conditioning further on a probability-zero nuisance value has no such bound,
which explains the stated limit of the approximation claim.
\end{proof}

The reference implementation uses the simultaneous finite-menu route. The selective Gaussian route is a theorem-level extension and requires the selection rule to be represented by a stable polyhedral or asymptotically equivalent event. Reusing $D_E$ without either route is not certified.

\section{Point targets and the fixed-\texorpdfstring{$x$}{x} boundary}\label{sec:fixedx}
The requested phrase “unconditional fixed-\(x\) coverage” has two meanings. Under a known, valid geometric envelope it is achievable. Under an unrestricted nonparametric class with an unknown envelope it is impossible uniformly.

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-\(x\) coverage under a known geometric envelope]\label{thm:fixedknown}
Fix a nonrandom query $x$ and condition on construction information. Suppose $x\in A$ and a deterministic bound
\[
 |m(x)-t_A|\le G_A(x)
\]
holds for every distribution in the declared model class. If $I_A$ has conditional coverage $1-\delta$ for $t_A$ from independent estimation data, then
\[
 I_A(x)=[\inf I_A-G_A(x),\ \sup I_A+G_A(x)]
\]
has conditional coverage at least $1-\delta$ for $m(x)$. This is unconditional after averaging over construction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On the event $t_A\in I_A$, the deterministic envelope places $m(x)$ in the displayed Minkowski expansion. Proposition~\ref{prop:tower} removes the conditioning.
\end{proof}
For a known Hölder constant and support radius, one may take $G_A(x)=2\epsilon_\phi+L\|\phi(x)-c_A\|^\alpha+L\rho_A^\alpha$. If the constant is estimated, its upper confidence event must be included in the failure probability.

\begin{theorem}[No uniformly shrinking fixed-$x$ interval without an identified bias modulus]\label{thm:fixedimpossible}
Consider a model class that imposes no common condition identifying the chosen
version of $m(x)$ from observations in a positive-mass neighbourhood of a fixed
query $x$; in particular, allow $P_X\{x\}=0$ and allow two admissible versions
to agree $P_X$-almost surely while differing at $x$. Then no finite-sample
interval can have uniform coverage $1-\delta$ for $m(x)$ and width converging
uniformly to zero when $\delta<1/2$. This obstruction does not apply to a
fixed Hölder ball with a regular design density, for which shrinking intervals
at the usual minimax rate are possible; it applies only when no common bias or
identification modulus is supplied.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Take two admissible versions $m_0,m_1$ that are equal $P_X$-almost surely but
satisfy $m_0(x)\ne m_1(x)$. They induce exactly the same distribution for every
finite sample, so the interval has the same law under both models. If its width
is smaller than $|m_1(x)-m_0(x)|$ with probability tending to one, it cannot
contain both target values. Hence the sum of the two failure probabilities is
at least $1-o(1)$, so their maximum has limit inferior at least $1/2$.
Uniform coverage above $1/2$ is therefore impossible. A Hölder or other
identified modulus rules out this version modification and leads instead to a
minimax rate theorem.\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[What an external anchor can and cannot prove]\label{cor:anchor}
An independent exchangeable anchor sample can calibrate a marginal geometric transport event and yield fresh-query coverage at level $1-\delta_R-\delta_G$. It does not yield uniform conditional coverage at every fixed $x$ unless the anchor event is itself uniform over the target covariate set.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply the conditional regional interval and intersect it with the anchor event, then use Proposition~\ref{prop:tower}. Uniform fixed-$x$ coverage would require the event to hold for all $x$ simultaneously, which is stronger than exchangeable marginal calibration.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Repeated-response anchor transport]\label{thm:anchor}
Freeze $t(x)$ and $s(x)\ge\eta_0>0$ independently of $A$ calibration anchors.
The calibration anchors and a future anchor, including their replicate counts
and conditionally independent responses in $[0,1]$ with mean $m(X_a)$, are
exchangeable. Use the same protocol and set
\[
 e_a=\sqrt{\log(2/\delta_A)/(2r_a)},\quad
 S_a=(|\overline Y_a-t(X_a)|+e_a)/s(X_a),\quad
 k=\lceil(A+1)(1-\delta_G)\rceil.
\]
Let $q=S_{(k)}$, with $q=+\infty$ if $k>A$. Then
$P\{|m(X_*)-t(X_*)|\le q s(X_*)\}\ge1-\delta_G-\delta_A$.
This is a marginal fresh-anchor guarantee, not fixed-covariate coverage.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Include the unobserved future replicates in the exchangeable experiment. With
ties treated conservatively, the rank argument gives $P(S_*\le q)\ge1-\delta_G$.
Conditional Hoeffding for the future anchor only gives
$P(|\overline Y_*-m(X_*)|\le e_*)\ge1-\delta_A$; no simultaneous calibration
mean event is asserted or needed. On the intersection the triangle inequality
gives the result. A union bound needs no independence of these two events.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Split-conformal geometric padding]\label{thm:conformalgeo}
Let a pilot predictor and selected regions be frozen independently of calibration anchors
$X_1^C,\ldots,X_{n_C}^C$ and a fresh query $X^\star$. Suppose a score $S(X)$ is constructed
from each anchor, and the calibration and fresh-query scores are exchangeable. If the score
dominance event
\[
 |m(X)-m(c_{A(X)})|\le S(X)\,
 \max\{\|\phi(X)-c_{A(X)}\|^\alpha,\epsilon_C\}
\]
holds for the calibration and query population, then the split-conformal quantile
$q_{1-\delta_G}$ gives
\[
 \mathbb P\!\left\{|m(X^\star)-m(c_{A(X^\star)})|
 \le q_{1-\delta_G}\max\{\|\phi(X^\star)-c_{A(X^\star)}\|^\alpha,\epsilon_C\}\right\}
 \ge1-\delta_G.
\]
The guarantee is marginal over $X^\star$. A dominance inequality valid at all $x$ does not turn a marginal score quantile into a fixed-$x$ guarantee; that requires a separate bound on $P\{S(x)\le q\}$ for that query or a simultaneous score envelope.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use rank $\lceil(n_C+1)(1-\delta_G)\rceil$ and $q=+\infty$ if it exceeds $n_C$. Under exchangeability the usual rank argument, with conservative handling of ties, shows that
the split-conformal quantile contains it with probability at least $1-\delta_G$. Apply the
dominance inequality to the fresh score and multiply by its known distance factor.
\end{proof}

\section{Group-level intervals and contamination}\label{sec:groups}
\begin{theorem}[Finite-sample group ratio interval]\label{thm:ratiofinite}
Under Definition~\ref{def:group}, let $0\le Z\le1$, so $0\le U_g\le V_g\le1$, $EV_g=\nu>0$, and define $\bar U,\bar V$. Put
\[
 e=\sqrt{\frac{\log(4/\delta)}{2B}},\quad
 I=\left[\frac{(\bar U-e)_+}{\bar V+e},
 \min\left\{1,\frac{\bar U+e}{\bar V-e}\right\}\right],
\]
with upper endpoint one when $\bar V\le e$. Then $\mathbb P(\theta\in I)\ge1-\delta$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Hoeffding gives each of $|\bar U-EU|\le e$ and $|\bar V-EV|\le e$ with failure probability at most $\delta/2$; a union bound therefore gives the simultaneous event with probability at least $1-\delta$. On that event, the smallest possible ratio is the lower endpoint and the largest possible ratio is the upper endpoint, using $EU\ge0$ and $EV\ge0$. The denominator safeguard handles $\bar V-e\le0$.
\end{proof}
For $K$ regions and $C$ outcomes replace $\delta$ by $\delta/(K(C+1))$. This is the exact finite-menu group-level route used by the current protocol.

\begin{proposition}[Observable frozen-cell contamination interval]\label{prop:contam}
Let $K$ cells be frozen independently of clean iid binary estimation outcomes.
In a cell with count $n_A>0$, at most $k_A$ labels are arbitrarily changed,
leaving the observed success count $\widetilde S_A$. Then
$|\widetilde S_A-S_A|\le k_A$. Set
$s_A^-=(\widetilde S_A-k_A)\vee0$ and
$s_A^+=(\widetilde S_A+k_A)\wedge n_A$. The observable interval
\[
 [L_{\rm CP}(s_A^-,n_A;\delta/K),U_{\rm CP}(s_A^+,n_A;\delta/K)]
\]
covers all clean regional probabilities simultaneously with probability at
least $1-\delta$. Use $[0,1]$ for empty cells. An alternative is
$[\widetilde S_A/n_A\pm\{\sqrt{\log(2K/\delta)/(2n_A)}+k_A/n_A\}]\cap[0,1]$.
If the budget holds only with probability $1-\delta_c$, subtract $\delta_c$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each changed label alters the success count by at most one, so
$s_A^-\le S_A\le s_A^+$. CP endpoints are monotone in the success count;
therefore the observable interval contains the clean CP interval on every
corruption realization. Conditional binomial coverage and a union bound prove
the claim. Clean-data Hoeffding and the count perturbation prove the alternative.
Adversarially corrupted outcomes need not be iid and have no assumed
``contaminated-cell population'' probability.\end{proof}
The bound is local. An unknown global noise rate does not provide a cell-specific budget; if labels regenerate the cells, a selection and geometry term must be added.

\section{Conditional extensions kept outside the flagship claim}
Quantile and causal region-to-target interfaces remain conditional extensions of the same point--region--point chain. They require, respectively, local density bounds and quantile transport, or unconfoundedness, overlap and cross-fitted nuisance rates. Their full derivations are archived in the evidence directory and are not used as evidence for the Nature flagship claims. The present SI retains the core finite-resolution, selection, independent-refit, group-level and geometric-transport results that are directly needed to interpret the experiments.

\section{Implementation, algorithms and honest boundaries}\label{sec:implementation}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fit the representation and candidate generator on $D_S$.
\item Generate a finite scale, shape and split menu; record parameter grids, boundary margins or soft temperatures.
\item Select risk and complexity on independent $D_V$.
\item Compute the regional table on $D_E$ or use an independent $D_R$ for refit.
\item Use exact binomial, bounded-score or group-ratio intervals for regional targets.
\item If a point target is reported, add a known geometric envelope or an independent anchor event.
\item If $D_E$ is reused to select the final index, construct simultaneous menu intervals or a valid selective law. Do not use an unadjusted regional interval.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Six-stage contract for label-informed candidates.}
The supervised extension is ordered so that task information can improve local resolution without making deployment label-dependent. First, a label-free geometry-first allocator fitted on $D_S$ defines a shared parent partition and its assignment fingerprint. Second, construction labels may generate only a finite candidate menu of class-aware seeds or task-conditioned distance terms; they do not directly set a query probability. Third, regional probabilities, out-of-fold residuals, point--region Brier regret, calibration, posterior width and effective/class-balanced support are estimated on independent calibration or validation roles. Fourth, a parent is eligible for local refinement only when its out-of-fold residual or boundary-mixing score is high and its support certificate passes; otherwise it is retained, routed to the point model or marked unsupported. Fifth, selection freezes the centres, cell IDs, covariate-only distance and deterministic tie rule, and deployment never reads a query outcome label. Sixth, the candidate is compared with the label-free headline through paired grouped bootstrap intervals and both assignment-preserving and reassignment-aware perturbation audits. This order is a protocol contract, not a guarantee of predictive improvement; a candidate that fails the gates is retained as a failure-boundary result.

The current archive audits the label-free OOF branch and the label-aware seed branch separately; it does not claim that a single integrated candidate has passed all six gates.

\paragraph{Allocator initialization is an explicit nuisance contract.}
When a regional allocation uses K-means or MiniBatchKMeans, the estimator,
initializer, number of starts, iteration/batch limits, random seed, scaling
and data order are recorded with the allocation. An optional stabilisation/audit
candidate may use deterministic $k$-means++ with an explicit multi-start value
($n_{\rm init}\geq5$ or $10$), followed by a fail-closed support check; canonical
reported runs follow their declared setting (here $n_{\rm init}=1$ where specified),
and larger multi-start counts are not treated as the primary accuracy intervention. Empty
or under-supported cells are merged, refined or marked unsupported before a
query is scored; they are not silently retained. If a finite initializer menu
is compared, the centres are fitted on $D_S$ and selection uses only a
pre-declared structure/validation rule, after which centres, cell IDs and an
assignment fingerprint are frozen. The supplied helper
\texttt{gbpe\_experiments.init\_policy.fit\_frozen\_allocator} writes this
contract and support diagnostics. This preserves the conditional partition
arguments because the realized partition is still a measurable function of
structure data and declared randomization. It does not give a predictive
accuracy or coverage guarantee: inertia and support are geometric criteria,
whereas Brier score, calibration and action recall depend on within-cell
response heterogeneity. A quick ten-split digits sensitivity audit is archived
in \texttt{evidence/initialization/}; it is descriptive and does not replace
the frozen STEAD, Solar or LHC headline runs. In that audit, full KMeans with
$k$-means++ changed mean Brier from $0.02096$ ($n_{\rm init}=1$) to $0.01933$
($n_{\rm init}=10$), whereas MiniBatchKMeans reached $0.02283$ but its mean
minimum support fell from $35.2$ to $20.6$. The result supports a stability
guard, not a universal precision claim.

\paragraph{Feature-weighted geometry as a finite audited candidate.}
The released Euclidean allocators use featurewise standardisation fitted on
the structure role and then assign by equal-weight squared distance in the
standardised coordinates,
\[
d_0^2(z,c)=\sum_{j=1}^{p}(z_j-c_j)^2 .
\]
This removes unit-scale dominance but does not assert that every transformed
feature, or every feature family, is equally predictive. A task-conditioned
candidate can instead use
\[
d_w^2(z,c)=\sum_{j=1}^{p}w_j(z_j-c_j)^2,\qquad
w_j\in[w_{\min},w_{\max}],\quad \frac1p\sum_{j=1}^{p}w_j=1,
\]
or a trace-normalised, shrinkage-regularised positive-semidefinite matrix
$M$ in $d_M^2(z,c)=(z-c)^\top M(z-c)$. Positive diagonal weights preserve a
deterministic nearest-centre equivalence-class assignment, but make the distance balls ellipsoidal and the induced Voronoi cells
task-conditioned in the original standardised coordinates; a
reusable allocation must therefore record the weight and metric version in its
fingerprint. We restrict the candidate menu to
feature-family weights, diagonal weights and a small low-rank/shrinkage menu;
unrestricted metric learning would make the allocation and its entropy depend
on an uncontrolled optimisation problem. Construction labels may propose the
menu or its starting weights, but fitting and selecting $w$ or $M$ uses only
declared calibration/validation roles. The selected metric, centres, cell IDs,
scale and tie rule are frozen before test reporting, and a reassignment-aware
audit is required in addition to the assignment-preserving audit. A weighted
metric can improve a proper score only when it reduces within-cell response
heterogeneity without collapsing support or inflating boundary instability;
therefore a favourable point estimate is insufficient. The promotion gate is
the same grouped-bootstrap non-inferiority interval, ECE tolerance, support
certificate and failure-rate check used for every regional candidate. The
current release records this as a metric-selection extension and does not
claim a weighted-distance accuracy gain unless those gates are passed.

\paragraph{Label-informed construction with label-free deployment.}
For a supervised task, a finite candidate menu may allocate $K_c$ seed
centres to class $c$, with $\sum_cK_c=K$, and fit those centres using the
construction observations from that class. Equal quotas and prevalence-
proportional quotas (for example, $2{:}1{:}1$) are candidate policies rather
than universally optimal rules: effective group support, multimodality and
local task heterogeneity may require a different allocation. The class of
origin is construction metadata only. Deployment assigns every query to the
pooled centres with one frozen covariate-only distance and tie rule; it never
uses the query's outcome label and does not force the regional probability to
zero or one. Class-count vectors and probabilities are refitted on the natural-
distribution independent estimation role, so class-balanced construction does
not silently change the probability estimand. Unrestricted pooled Lloyd
updates and quota-preserving prototypes are distinct candidates because the
former can erase the initial class allocation. A label-informed candidate is
accepted only after its allocation fingerprint, support certificate, Brier,
log-loss, ECE and failure rates pass the same independent validation and
grouped-bootstrap gates as the label-free headline allocator.

\paragraph{Solar label-quota probe.}
A small $K=64$ diagnostic tested prevalence-proportional seeds for the rare-event
Solar task. Structure labels formed class-wise centres, probabilities were
refitted on an independent calibration role, selection was separate, and test
assignment used pooled frozen centres without query labels. The proportional
quota changed test Brier from $0.022309$ to $0.023239$ (24 h) and from
$0.033789$ to $0.034793$ (48 h), versus point references $0.016955/0.024885$;
the paired HARPNUM intervals for the supervised initialisation minus point were
$[0.00417,0.00676]$ and $[0.00546,0.00940]$. ECE remained above point at both
horizons ($0.01304/0.01474$ versus $0.00546/0.00548$). The rule therefore
over-specialises rare-event centres in this split and is retained as a failure
boundary, not an accuracy claim or a replacement for the released label-free
allocator. Rows and metadata are in
\texttt{solar\_supervised\_init\_probe\_{metrics,bootstrap,meta}.*}.

\paragraph{Task-conditioned feature-weight probe.}
A role-separated Solar audit compared the released standardized equal-weight
metric with structure-only inverse-noise and diagonal supervised weights. The
latter used group-balanced class-separation effects, a fixed shrinkage menu
$\eta\in\{0.25,0.50,1.00\}$ and bounded weights; calibration estimated cell
probabilities and validation selected the candidate. At 24 h the selected
$\eta=1$ gave Brier/ECE $0.020527/0.02262$ versus $0.022874/0.01369$ for
equal weights; at 48 h the selected $\eta=0.50$ gave $0.029698/0.02427$
versus $0.033859/0.01263$. The grouped (one mean per HARPNUM, rather than row-weighted)
intervals for weighted region minus point were $[0.00163,0.00306]$ and $[0.00215,0.00434]$;
point Brier/ECE were $0.016955/0.00546$ and $0.024885/0.00548$.
Digits showed a smaller equal-weight regional improvement (mean Brier
$0.00651\rightarrow0.00642$ over five seeds), but remained above point
(about $0.00635$) with higher ECE and a zero-crossing interval. Automatic
weights therefore remain a task-conditioned geometry diagnostic, not a
headline accuracy claim. Scripts, tables and role metadata are in
\texttt{evidence/initialization/\{solar,digits\}\_feature\_weight\_probe\_*}.

\subsection{GB-native allocator and fail-closed audit}\label{sec:gb_native_audit}
The cross-domain tables use KMeans- or MiniBatchKMeans-backed allocation cells to keep the regional-object interface fixed across data sources. To verify that the Granular Ball identity is executable rather than only an abstraction, a separate bounded audit uses the released recursive \texttt{GranularBallProbabilityEstimator}. It fits deterministic coordinate-median splits on a 60\% structure role, leaves 20\% of the training rows unused, and evaluates on 7,200 independently generated test rows. Terminal balls are eligible only when calibration support is at least 30, the median nearest--second-nearest-centre margin is at least 0.05, the query lies within the terminal radius, and the normal-approximation upper confidence bound for regional-minus-point Brier regret is non-positive. Any failed query is routed to point Logistic.

The GB-native-S head keeps the continuous point surface and adds a support-shrunk, distance-attenuated local logit residual; GB-native-H is the hard regional control. On 12 fixed seeds with no label flipping, 8.3\% of terminal balls and 9.5\% of test queries passed the gate; S had mean Brier 0.19740 versus 0.19829 for point Logistic (difference $-0.00089$). These seed summaries are descriptive implementation evidence, not coverage intervals, external validation or a universal accuracy result. The runner, metadata, and summary are \texttt{evidence/gb\_native/run\_gb\_native\_allocator\_audit.py}, \texttt{gb\_native\_allocator\_meta.json} and \texttt{gb\_native\_allocator\_summary.csv}.

A real-domain extension was then run on the checksum-verified Zenodo 12,000-window STEAD subset (2,381 station-hash holdout windows; the raw HDF5 inputs are external and are not redistributed). With the same recursive allocator and certificate, 1/64 terminal balls passed and only 3.61\% of holdout queries used a regional route; the remaining 96.39\% fell back to point Logistic. Point Brier was $0.028269$, GB-native-S was $0.028312$ (difference $+0.000043$), and the hard regional control was $0.028207$ (difference $-0.000062$). This is a real-domain allocator and failure-boundary audit: the soft head did not improve accuracy, the hard-control difference is descriptive and based on a small certified subset, and neither result is promoted as a general STEAD or resource claim. Input hashes, certificates, metrics and runtime receipt are recorded in \texttt{evidence/stead/stead\_gb\_native\_\{status,metrics,certificates,runtime\_receipt\}.\{json,csv\}}; the canonical KMeans-backed STEAD action table is unchanged.

\paragraph{Dedicated GB--Logistic--S perturbation stress.}

A complete table regeneration requires the full cited SWAN archive; the included download script supports deterministic subset verification.\\par

\textbf{Physical object and target.} The raw computational object is an ordered HARPNUM activity-region time window. A Granular Ball $A$ is a measurable state-space region whose members have similar magnetic-flux and field-strength proxies (for example \texttt{USFLUX}, \texttt{R\_VALUE}, \texttt{TOTFZ}), shear and polarity-inversion-line proxies (\texttt{MEANSHR}, \texttt{SHRGT45}), helicity/current proxies (\texttt{TOTUSJH}, \texttt{TOTUSJZ}, \texttt{ABSNJZH}, \texttt{MEANJZH}, \texttt{MEANJZD}, \texttt{MEANALP}), field-gradient/complexity proxies (\texttt{MEANGAM}, \texttt{MEANGBT}, \texttt{MEANGBZ}, \texttt{MEANGBH}, \texttt{SAVNCPP}) and the two-hour slopes of these variables. For $h\in\{24,48\}$, the regional target is
\[
p_A^{(h)}=\Pr\{Y_{M+}^{(h)}=1\mid X\in A\}.
\]
The regional quality record is task-conditioned rather than purely geometric. Its first component is $n_{A,\mathrm{eff}}=N_{\rm eff}(A)$, the valid, de-duplicated HARPNUM--time-window support; its supervised components include positive/negative counts, class-balanced support, the Laplace-smoothed response probability, within-region heterogeneity, posterior-width or score-regret diagnostics, and the resulting refine/retain/fallback status. The geometry record stores the standardized centre, declared norm/shape, scale, an optional principal direction and 95\% support boundary (the frozen $K=64$ geometry is in \texttt{solar\_ball\_geometry\_k64.csv}). The released anchor construction is unique at the HARPNUM--anchor-time key; the audit additionally checks this key before fitting. This is a magnetic-state geometry, not a solar-surface or causal magnetic-topology claim. Support and labels are therefore part of the task certificate, while the frozen assignment remains a separately auditable reuse object. The source description above does not replace the deployment action estimand $\theta_{A,D}^{(h)}$, which uses the target time-window measure.

The promoted clean adaptive hierarchy retains 17/18 balls and records 2/4 child-trigger rows at 24/48 h; the current sensitivity runner counts these as 1/2 parent split events. The two counters are retained with their definitions, and neither is a claim that the hierarchy is optimal. The machine-readable audit records each parent trigger or stop reason in \texttt{solar\_24h\_adaptive\_ball\_audit.csv}, \texttt{solar\_48h\_adaptive\_ball\_audit.csv} and \texttt{solar\_adaptive\_summary.json}.

\textbf{Prediction target and leakage control.} The released labels mark whether a localized M1.0-or-higher flare occurs strictly after the anchor and no later than $h$ hours. X+ is a secondary sparse stratum and is never used for model, threshold or refinement selection. Each eligible state contains 24 transformed magnetic parameters and two-hour slopes, requires finite values, \texttt{QUALITY=0}, \texttt{IS\_TMFI=False}, two hours of history and 48 hours of subsequent records. The implementation emits one 12-h anchor per eligible time step, not one anchor per HARPNUM. All states from a HARPNUM inherit the split of its first eligible observation: structure through 2012, validation in 2013 and test from 2014 onward. The table has 27,745 states (9,902 structure, 5,184 validation and 12,659 test) from 2,630 HARPNUMs; the test contains 1,205 HARPNUMs, 304/483 M+ positive states and 29/48 X+ positive states at 24/48 h. \textbf{Matched-budget protocol.} The primary online unit is $g=(d,\mathrm{HARPNUM})$, one activity region visible on one UTC date. At budget fraction $b$, the first $\lceil b|G_d|\rceil$ units are escalated. Flat regional curves therefore diagnose a cached-state contract, not online robustness under corrupted-feature reassignment. \textbf{Problem definition and canonical representation.} The collider task is defined as resource-aware compression: without pre-specifying a new-physics model, can $10^6$ collision events be reduced to a small set of phase-space cells carrying enough event mass, geometric concentration, sideband support and sideband-model tail evidence to justify a physics fit or significance test? The first 70\% of the event order is an unlabeled development stream and the final 30\% is a held-out audit stream. The derived event vector includes multiplicity, $H_T$, leading and subleading $p_T$, all-particle mass, transverse recoil, weighted $\eta$ dispersion and circular $\phi$ concentration. The canonical allocation coordinates, fixed by \texttt{lhco\_split\_index.json}, are
\[
z=(\log(1+m_{j,1}),\log(1+p_{T,j_1}),\log(1+m_{\rm all})),
\]
standardized using development moments only. A Granular Ball is a static phase-space allocation cell, not an arbitrary embedding-space ball. The protocol record stores event mass, background support, centre, declared norm and radius, support boundary, an anomaly interval, sideband-transfer uncertainty and an eligibility or no-fit status; the measured raw-event table exports support, radius and anti-$k_T$-derived event geometry. The released table has a single $K=64$ static partition and no parent--child split trace; it is therefore not promoted as a completed adaptive hierarchy.

\textbf{Sidebands, baselines and multiplicity.} For a cell $A$, $n_{\rm on,A}$ is the audit count in the frozen mass window and $n_{\rm sb,A}$ is the count in adjacent sidebands. A development-only transfer factor gives $B_A$; the local tail is a Poisson null tail with its sideband uncertainty carried into the interval. The point baseline is a development-frozen Isolation Forest score. The conventional baseline scans the same one-dimensional all-particle-mass windows and sideband rule. The historical scan uses the fixed 64-cell menu and 314 mass windows, hence 378 region/window trials. The raw-event budget audit uses the 64-cell regional menu and keeps the historical scan separate. The released MiniBatchKMeans allocation uses one declared start ($n_{\rm init}=1$) and seed, so the canonical LHC table is a frozen single-start audit; no multi-start initialization robustness or restart-averaged candidate count is claimed. The single-layer audit has no data-driven split calls; refinement is a predeclared future extension. The historical scan reports a Bonferroni correction and a 500-resample empirical maximum-statistic pilot. Neither supplies a validated global-discovery $p$-value for the raw-event rerun, whose extreme local tails require sideband-closure and multiplicity checks.

\section{Public STEAD seismic waveform audit}\label{sec:stead}

The local MD5 values are \texttt{d3123e11abce4b3e0fa96b3d6228110b} for \texttt{test.hdf5} and \texttt{b94ca2ccf9b26f9d3c645d5b86e66a42} for \texttt{test\_noise.hdf5}. \textbf{Regional object and estimand.} The raw unit is a one-minute three-component waveform. The STEAD regional allocation is a bounded waveform cell defined from twelve frozen amplitude, spectral, duration and phase-structure descriptors; catalogue P/S pick fields are not used as representation inputs.  p_A=\Pr\{Y_{\rm earthquake}\mid X\in A\},
\]
with effective support, noise fraction, standardized centre and radius, and a support/refinement state recorded in \texttt{stead\_raw\_region\_states.csv}. \textbf{Grouped split and raw rerun.} All 12,000 waveforms are read from the HDF5 arrays before feature extraction. \section{Frozen-allocation reuse and invalidation}
\label{sec:frozenreuse}
A regional record is reusable only while its allocation and support contract remains valid. The following elementary result makes the action boundary explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Frozen-allocation reuse certificate]\label{thm:frozenreuse}
Let $A$ be a measurable allocation cell, let $T_A=\E[\psi(Y)\mid X\in A]$, and let $\widehat T_A$ be computed from observations assigned to $A$ under a frozen allocation rule $\Pi$. Suppose a new query distribution preserves the conditional response law on $A$, has effective support at least $n_A^{\min}$, and remains inside the declared geometric envelope. If the stored estimator has error certificate $|\widehat T_A-T_A|\leq b_A$ with failure probability at most $\delta_A$, then reusing the stored probability state for any number of queries satisfying these conditions retains the same conditional certificate. If any condition fails, the certificate does not transport; the record must be recomputed, refined or marked unsupported.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the stated conditional-law, support and geometric-envelope conditions, every admissible query uses the same target $T_A$ and the same allocation event. The stored error event therefore remains the event $|\widehat T_A-T_A|\leq b_A$, whose conditional probability is at least $1-\delta_A$. When a condition fails, either the target law, the denominator or the point-to-region transport remainder changes; the original event no longer bounds the new target without an additional transport term. This is exactly the stated invalidation rule.\end{proof}

The result is a contract theorem rather than a universal caching guarantee. It formalizes why assignment-preserving perturbations can audit a cached state, while feature-driven reassignment, support loss, boundary crossing or uncontrolled transport requires recomputation or an unsupported status.

\paragraph{Four-layer quality certificate and refinement stop.}
To test the proposed precision-oriented region quality measure, we added a hard certificate before any continuous quality ranking. For each frozen $K=64$ cell the certificate records (i) HARPNUM-level effective and class-balanced support, (ii) a cluster-aware working posterior width, (iii) support-relative position $u=d/\max(\rho_{\rm rob},\rho_{\min})$ and signed envelope margin $g=1-u$, (iv) a query-level assignment margin and perturbation ratio, and (v) a late-selector region--point Brier regret with a one-sided grouped bootstrap upper bound. Here $\rho_{\rm rob}$ is a structure-role empirical distance quantile; it describes the observed envelope and is not a population-support guarantee. The support envelope and the nearest-centre allocation boundary are separate events. For a common frozen metric $M$, a query assigned to $j$ has
\[
b_{\rm ass}(x)=\min_{k\ne j}\frac{d_k(x)^2-d_j(x)^2}{2\sqrt{(c_k-c_j)^\top M(c_k-c_j)}},
\qquad r_{\rm stab}(x)=b_{\rm ass}(x)/\varepsilon_{\rm budget}(x),
\]
where metric-identical centres and ties receive zero margin. For a general declared norm we use the conservative bound $(d_{(2)}-d_{(1)})/2$. A margin larger than the perturbation budget is the assignment-preserving condition; otherwise the row is routed to fallback or reassignment-aware recomputation. These fields are computed by the frozen-assignment helper in \texttt{source\_archive/gbpe\_experiments/geometry\_certificates.py}; it never repairs a stale assignment. The posterior interval is a working uncertainty summary, not an exact beta-binomial coverage statement under serial dependence. A reassignment-aware stability floor is recorded separately; assignment-preserving perturbations keep the allocation fixed and perturb only posterior or refinement state. A cell can contribute a regional probability only when all hard conditions pass and its regret upper bound is no larger than the predeclared non-inferiority tolerance. Otherwise the action is an explicit point fallback.

The same certificate controls one-step refinement. A parent is considered for a local principal-direction split only when support, width and boundary conditions are met. The child-minus-parent Brier difference must have a grouped bootstrap upper bound below zero, and the children must also pass the child-versus-point regret guard, class-balanced support and stability conditions. We impose one split per parent, depth one and a finite split budget; a rejected candidate is retained with a stop reason rather than silently discarded. This is a refinement state of the regional object, not a new unconstrained clustering run.

On the Solar retrospective test replay, the corrected certificate accepted zero of 64 cells at both horizons and accepted no candidate split. The selected point anchors therefore remained unchanged: Brier $0.016621/0.024519$ and ECE $0.003816/0.005138$ at 24/48 h. The regional contribution and split fractions were both 0\%, and the actual point-call fraction was 100\%. The bootstrap regret upper bounds were close to zero for a few cells, but no cell passed all support, posterior-width, boundary and temporal-persistence conditions. This result is a conservative fallback diagnostic; because the same released Solar test stream has been repeatedly examined during development, it is not fresh confirmatory evidence that every regional estimator or split fails. Machine-readable cell certificates, split diagnostics, predictions and paired bootstrap are in \texttt{evidence/next\_upgrade/solar\_accuracy\_upgrade/solar\_quality\_certificate\_*.csv} and \texttt{solar\_quality\_certificate\_meta.json}. The corrected interpretation strengthens the audit contract: support, posterior width, geometry, regret, stability and fallback are explicit, while statistical invalidity and conservative abstention are distinguished from an impossibility claim.

\subsection{Fixed-assignment scale, boundary and residual routing audit}
\label{sec:fixedscaleaudit}
The five new quantities were introduced as quality-audit and routing variables while the released allocation was held fixed. For a query assigned to cell $j$, let $d_j$ be its distance to the frozen centre in the declared common metric and let $R_j$ be the structure-role empirical support radius. We record the relative support position $u_j=d_j/(R_j\vee R_{\min})$ and the signed support-envelope value $1-u_j$; this is a scale diagnostic, not a population support guarantee. It is distinct from the distance to the actual allocation boundary. For a shared positive-definite metric, after the corresponding whitening, the exact nearest-centre margin is
\[
b_{\rm ass}(z)=\min_{k\ne j}
 \frac{\|z-c_k\|^2-\|z-c_j\|^2}{2\|c_k-c_j\|},
\qquad
r_{\rm stab}(z)=b_{\rm ass}(z)/(\epsilon(z)\vee\epsilon_{\min}),
\]
where the minimum is over all competing centres, ties have zero margin, and $\epsilon$ is the declared perturbation budget in the same metric. The conservative common-norm alternative $(d_{(2)}-d_{(1)})/2$ is used only when the exact common-metric expression is unavailable. Neither quantity implies probability accuracy. Distinct per-cell metrics and a child split introduce additional score boundaries and therefore require their own perturbation calculation.

The quality gate is applied after probability estimation. On the independent calibration role we record the cell-level point--region Brier regret and a one-sided working upper score $U_A$; its zero-variance or $z=0$ branch is explicitly diagnostic rather than a finite-sample confidence certificate. A route may use the regional probability only when support, class-balanced support, posterior-width/calibration, boundary and $U_A$ gates pass; otherwise it records a failure reason and calls the point model. A change from $d$ to $d/R$ is therefore not silently used to redefine membership. If it is ever used to select a different cell, the probability table, allocation fingerprint, grouped bootstrap and frozen version must all be regenerated, with a reassignment-aware audit.

Every candidate record now carries the metric identifier and weights, centre and radius quantile, support and class counts, allocation-boundary definition and margin, perturbation budget and ratio, calibration regret and $U_A$, assignment fingerprint, failure reason and fallback action. The query-level prediction table separately records whether a point forward was actually invoked. A full point reference is reported as a benchmark and is never counted as a route call. This separates logical fallback flags from real point calls and preserves the central object as a reusable, auditable, failure-detectable probability record.

\subsection{Fixed-partition probability-surface ablation}
\label{sec:ballsurface}

The regional interface does not require the regional predictor to be a lookup table. We therefore tested a model-side extension while preserving the central contract. A single standardized $K=32$, $n_{\rm init}=1$ MiniBatch-$k$-means allocation was fit on the Solar structure role and reused unchanged by every head. The hard cell ID, centre, radius and support were held fixed within this ablation; no query label entered the assignment. A complete deployment certificate containing the scaler, centre matrix, head coefficients, margins and perturbation state is not claimed by this probe and must be added before treating the surface as a reusable release object. The late selector HARPNUMs chose the regularization constant; the nominal calibration role was left unused in this probe, and the released test stream was opened once for the scores and paired bootstrap.

For binary response, let $z$ denote the frozen standardized representation, $c_j$ the cell centre and $R_j$ the structure-role 95th-percentile distance. The four nonconstant heads use a regularized logistic surface with the following additional basis terms: a global logit (M1); a hard-cell intercept (M2); a hard-cell intercept and four local slopes (M3); and compact-support Wendland weights
\[
\kappa(u)=(1-u)_+^4(4u+1),\qquad
S(z)=\sum_k\kappa(\|z-c_k\|/(R_k+\epsilon_R)),\qquad
\widetilde w_j(z)=\frac{\kappa(\|z-c_j\|/(R_j+\epsilon_R))}{\max\{S(z),\epsilon_S\}}
\]
with local slopes (M4). Here $\epsilon_R$ and $\epsilon_S$ are fixed numerical floors recorded with the run. The implementation uses the displayed denominator floor: when $0<S(z)<\epsilon_S$, the local terms are attenuated rather than renormalised, and when $S(z)=0$ they vanish while the shared global term remains. These low-mass rows are recorded as a coverage diagnostic; they are not counted as a point-model call. M4 is a prediction basis over the frozen regions, not a new allocator or a label-informed reassignment. Its local slope uses the four predeclared Solar coordinates $R\_VALUE\_now$, $TOTUSJH\_now$, $TOTBSQ\_now$ and $TOTPOT\_now$; this is a fixed low-dimensional head choice, not a test-selected feature claim. The four-dimensional local slope is a deliberately small regularized surface; it is not a claim that a ball is intrinsically differentiable at a hard assignment boundary. The current finite-floor implementation should be read as continuous/attenuated on the frozen representation, with no global $C^1$ or hard-boundary smoothness claim.

\paragraph{Nested GB--Logistic head and requested H/S aliases.}
The requested probability-head extension was evaluated without refitting or changing the allocation. Let $a(z)$ be the frozen hard cell, $c_j$ its centre and $w_j(z)$ the fixed compact-support Wendland weight. The hard-local comparator and soft surface can be written
\[
\eta_{\rm hard}(z)=\eta_0(z)+b_{a(z)}+u_{a(z)}^\top(z-c_{a(z)}),\qquad
\eta_{\rm soft}(z)=\eta_0(z)+\sum_jw_j(z)\{b_j+u_j^\top(z-c_j)\},
\]
where $\eta_0$ is the global M1 point-logistic head. Setting every $b_j$ and $u_j$ to zero recovers $\eta_0$ exactly, establishing the nested relation within the frozen head family. It does not establish containment of the historical legacy point-anchor path, which used a different representation and regularisation-selection history.

For naming, the canonical ablation retains M3 for the hard-local head and M4 for the soft Wendland head. The requested hybrid alias was then implemented as $\mathrm{GB\text{-}Logistic\text{-}H}_{\rm hybrid}$: M4's soft residual plus a frozen hard-cell support/audit/fallback block. \subsection{Unified seven-direction regional probability contract}
\label{sec:seven_direction_contract}

The seven directions are implementation layers of one regional probability model, rather than seven interchangeable predictors. The contract first freezes a representation and a parent allocation, then estimates a local probability surface and its regional estimand, and finally decides whether that surface is admissible for reuse or must fall back to the point model. For a binary response, the intended decomposition is
\[
\ell_A(x)=\ell_0(\phi(x))+\gamma_A r_A(\phi(x)),\qquad
\widehat\theta_{A,D}=\int_A\widehat q_A(x)\,d\widehat\mu_{A,D}^{\rm deploy}(x),
\]
where $r_A$ is estimated with out-of-fold parent--child residuals and $\gamma_A$ is set to zero whenever the support, posterior-width or one-sided Brier-regret gate fails. The true target is $\theta_{A,D}=\int_A m_D(x)\,d\mu_{A,D}^{\rm deploy}(x)$; the displayed integral is its estimated counterpart. Replacing the conditional surface by a constant cell posterior is a deliberately tested failure boundary. A graph field, adaptive split and multi-horizon hazard constraint are optional operators on this same record and must preserve its allocation fingerprint and fallback semantics.

Table~\ref{tab:seven_direction_contract} records the status of each layer. Every candidate is fitted on the structure role, selected on an independent calibration/validation role and evaluated once on the outer test role. The grouped bootstrap unit follows the task dependence (HARPNUM, subject, seed, station or event group). A candidate enters the main accuracy or cost claim only when its paired interval satisfies the prespecified Brier non-inferiority/improvement gate, ECE and coverage tolerances, and, for routing, a real-call or wall-time criterion. A failed candidate is retained as an auditable region record with its failure reason and point fallback; its point estimate is not promoted.

r_A=1.96\sqrt{V_w(q_A)/n_{\mathrm{eff},A}}+1.96s_{\mathrm{cal},A}/\sqrt{n_{\mathrm{cal},A}}+L_AW_1^{\mathrm{coord}}(\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{str},A},\widehat\mu_{\mathrm{cal},A}),

\subsection{Coupled deployment-proxy and continuous-surface experiment}
A new exploratory screen used the same frozen physical point representation and $K=32$ allocation. A two-coordinate domain classifier estimated a clipped covariate density ratio toward the late-selector distribution. The ratio entered both the local weighted logistic-offset likelihood and the regional integration weights, with interpolation $\alpha\in\{0,0.5,1\}$. Local intercepts and two centered coordinates received a support- and posterior-width-conditioned ridge prior; three-fold HARPNUM local OOF chose among three prior strengths. The late selector compared the fitted query surfaces against the unchanged physical point fallback. This is a coupled plug-in estimator with shared weights, not simultaneous identification of an unknown future deployment law. The prior widths are regularization proxies, not binomial coverage certificates for dependent observations.

Both horizons selected the physical point fallback. Query Brier was therefore $0.016629/0.024519$, ECE $0.003288/0.005138$, and log loss $0.059861/0.086896$ at 24/48 h, with paired HARPNUM-bootstrap Brier changes and intervals exactly zero ($B=1000$). This demonstrates rejection of the tested correction, not a regional accuracy gain. Broadcasting the selected region integral to individual queries remained worse by $+0.004257[+0.002057,+0.007056]$ and $+0.007186[+0.004003,+0.011113]$. These are deliberately mismatched-resolution diagnostics, not tests proving measure error. Region-level estimates are separately compared with matched point integrals and observed regional frequencies.

A companion screen that changed only the density-ratio target from calibration to late-selector covariates selected zero reweighting at both horizons. Its deployment-proxy regional Brier was $0.021247/0.032233$ versus $0.016629/0.024519$ for physical point at 24/48 h; paired differences were $+0.004617[+0.001910,+0.007971]$ and $+0.007714[+0.003823,+0.012061]$. Thus moving the proxy window alone did not resolve the problem. The source--target density ratio is fitted without target outcomes; target labels are used only for selector decisions. Both screens reused the historical test stream, and reporting its year strata is not a fresh outer-time validation. Global point regularization had used calibration labels, so the local OOF analysis is conditional on that head rather than a fully nested OOF guarantee. No real routing or end-to-end cost advantage is promoted.

\paragraph{Deployment estimand and identification.}
The sample-level output is $\widehat q_A(x)$; the regional output is $\theta_{A,D}=\int_A m_D(x)\,d\mu_{A,D}^{\rm deploy}(x)$. A predeployment window estimates a proxy for $\mu_{A,D}^{\rm deploy}$, not the true future measure. Under covariate shift, support overlap and an invariant conditional law, normalized density-ratio integration is admissible. If the conditional law changes, unlabeled target covariates alone cannot identify $m_D$; new labeled calibration or explicit sensitivity assumptions are required. The record therefore stores the target population, target window, weighting convention, ratio clipping, effective support and drift/fallback state.

\paragraph{Next discriminating experiments.}
First compare fixed-measure surface models and fixed-surface measure estimators on matched estimands. Then test group-balanced direct target-window quadrature and a cross-fitted residual-corrected integral; neither requires a complex density model as the first baseline. Replace the heuristic width by a group-valid mean-error interval plus an explicit drift sensitivity term. Only then test supervised local geometry or splits with separate child support and heterogeneity criteria. Requiring every child interval to be narrower than its parent can reject useful refinements; the decision should compare total predictive risk and separately enforce an uncertainty tolerance. Further development uses historical data as development data; a genuinely unexamined temporal cohort is needed for confirmation. Evidence: \texttt{coupled\_deployment\_*.csv/json}, \texttt{deployment\_measure\_*.csv/json} and \texttt{README\_JOINT\_SURFACE\_MEASURE\_CN.md} in the regional-model evidence directory.

\paragraph{Orthogonal regional estimand as the next precision target.}
For a region-level action, the next estimator should target the deployment quantity directly rather than broadcast a regional mean to individual samples. With a source calibration role $S$, a target covariate window $T$ and a cross-fitted surface $\widehat m$, a doubly robust candidate is
\[
 \widehat\theta_{A,D}^{\rm DR} =
 \frac{1}{|T_A|}\sum_{j\in T_A}\widehat m(X_j)
 +
 \frac{\sum_{i\in S_A}\widehat w(X_i)\{Y_i-\widehat m(X_i)\}}
      {\sum_{i\in S_A}\widehat w(X_i)} .
\]
Here $\widehat w$ is a clipped, normalized density ratio estimated without target labels. The estimator is admissible only with cross-fitting by time and HARPNUM, positive overlap, a recorded effective sample size and a target-window label or an explicit sensitivity model. Its uncertainty must be obtained from HARPNUM/time-group influence contributions, not iid row resampling. If either the surface or the density ratio is consistently estimated, the first-order nuisance error can be reduced; if neither is identified, the output remains a sensitivity interval and the region falls back to the point surface. The experiment should report action-level proper scores or fixed-budget utility for $\theta_{A,D}$ and sample-level Brier/ECE for $\widehat q_A(x)$ separately.

\subsection{Staged hierarchical probability-model upgrade}
\label{sec:staged_hierarchical_upgrade}
The precision-directed screens motivate a stricter staged model rather than another increase in the number of balls. The physical representation is frozen first, so any subsequent change is an incremental regional effect. Inside each parent region we then fit
\[
 \widehat q_A(x)=\sigma\!\left(f_{\rm phys}(\phi(x))+b_A+u_A^\top(\phi(x)-c_A)\right),
 \qquad
 \widehat\theta_{A,D}=\int_A\widehat q_A(x)\,d\widehat\mu_{A,D}^{\rm deploy}(x),
\]
where $f_{\rm phys}$ is the frozen point surface, $(b_A,u_A)$ is a low-rank residual, and $\widehat\mu_A^{\rm deploy}$ is estimated separately from the structure and calibration measures. The local parameter covariance is a support-conditioned prior,
\[
 (b_A,u_A)\sim\mathcal N\!\left(0,\Sigma_A\{\operatorname{ESS}_{A}^{\rm bal},w_A,\rho_A\}\right),
\]
with class-balanced/dependence-adjusted effective support, posterior width $w_A$ and within-region heterogeneity $\rho_A$. The prior is fitted on the calibration role; it is not a post-hoc safe-cell switch.

The reported regional integral must carry the joint radius
\[
 U_A=U_{\rm surface,A}+U_{\rm measure,A}+U_{\rm MC,A},
\]
where the first term is the cross-fitted surface residual, the second is the coordinate-transport or density-ratio remainder and the third is finite-measure Monte Carlo error. A region is eligible for supervised local splitting only if its OOF residual heterogeneity and boundary mixing exceed their calibration thresholds, its class-balanced support passes, and the child-level $U_A$ is no wider than the parent-level certificate. Otherwise the parent surface or point model is retained.

The staged protocol is therefore: (i) freeze the enhanced physical point representation; (ii) estimate the global-plus-local surface; (iii) estimate the deployment measure and $U_A$ jointly; (iv) learn the support/width prior; (v) test supervised splits only in high-heterogeneity cells; (vi) repeat the complete choice on one outer Solar time block; and (vii) only after these gates pass, measure risk-gated routing with real end-to-end calls and wall time. The current lightweight evidence does not yet pass the regional-integral gate: the joint surface--measure probe worsened integrated Brier by $+0.004617[+0.002078,+0.007770]$ and $+0.007714[+0.003963,+0.011960]$ at 24/48 h, and the support prior selected zero residual amplitude at both horizons. These results motivate investigation of local residual estimation, aggregation loss and measure mismatch; they do not identify a unique cause of failure. The staged order prevents a routing or splitting gain from being attributed to a regional probability surface that has not yet been certified.

For clarity, the implementation is a seven-plus-one contract rather than an unordered collection of upgrades. The seven pre-routing operations are: (1) freeze the validated physical representation and parent allocation; (2) retain the sample-level conditional surface $\widehat q_A(x)$ and the region-level action estimand separately; (3) estimate $\mu_{A,D}^{\mathrm{deploy}}$ on a development/calibration role and record overlap, clipping and effective support; (4) fit a physical baseline plus a low-rank within-region residual; (5) use effective/class-balanced support and posterior width as a prior on local update precision; (6) refine only high-heterogeneity, sufficiently supported boundary regions; and (7) accept a regional output only when its total risk and joint surface--measure uncertainty pass the predeclared gate. The ``plus one'' operation is a risk-gated region-first route evaluated with real point calls and end-to-end cost, and is enabled only after the seven checks pass. The region graph and joint hazard map are optional auxiliary operators in this order. The current lightweight evidence fails at operation (7), so operation (8) remains a measured routing failure boundary rather than a promoted efficiency result.

\subsection{Finite probability-head registry and promotion gates}
\label{sec:head_registry}
The allocation contract is independent of the probability head; a registered head keeps the point surface, declares its modification and zero-radius limit, and exposes the shared support, boundary, transport, action and fallback fields. The P5 runner checks \texttt{HEAD\_REGISTRY.csv/.json}, zero-radius, fallback and ledger alignment; it performs no head search, fitting or promotion.

The registry separates the statistical abstraction from its implementation. A \emph{regional probability object} is the abstract object with a declared estimand, effective support, uncertainty, geometry, reuse conditions, action state and failure state. A Granular Ball is the concrete auditable geometric unit used here to instantiate that object: its centre, radius, assignment fingerprint, support record, radial/Voronoi margins and refinement path make those fields inspectable and replayable. This distinction gives the framework scope beyond one link function without reducing Granular Ball to a generic cluster, bin or cache. The same contract creates the operational chain
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{regional object}\longrightarrow\text{certificate}\longrightarrow
\{\text{reuse, compression, stability}\}\\
\longrightarrow\{\text{refine, re-certify, point fallback, abstain}\}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The last implication is conditional: a certified region may be reused or counted in an object/call budget only under the declared support, homogeneity, boundary, calibration, deployment-measure and drift conditions. A failed certificate contributes an explicit failure state and cannot be silently reused.

The complete zero-radius closure is semantic rather than a radius label attached to an existing multi-point cell. Under singleton/identity allocation, prediction, the Logistic objective, the Dirac regional estimand, proper-score and calibration functionals, action state, certificate status and fallback route reduce to the point Logistic counterparts. Zero local coefficients alone provide point-surface and objective (and corresponding proper-score/calibration) equality; point action, certificate and fallback equality requires the declared zero-radius identity branch or an explicitly rejected local gate. Thus the registry records a point-compatible computational family rather than a separate regional score appended to a point model.

The canonical head is GB--Logistic--S. For a frozen cell $A_j$, it takes the form
\[
 \eta_S(z)=\eta_{\rm point}(z)+g_j\gamma_j r_j(z),\qquad z\in A_j,
\]
where $g_j$ is a calibration-role gate and $\gamma_j$ is a support- and uncertainty-controlled local weight. The point family is exactly embedded:
\[
 \text{point Logistic}\subset\text{GB--Logistic--S},
 \qquad g_j\gamma_j=0\ \forall j\Longrightarrow\widehat p_S(z)=\widehat p_{\rm point}(z).
\]
GB--Logistic--H is a hard support/audit/fallback control. It exposes cell support, geometry, boundary and failure state, but is not treated as the canonical row-level probability surface. This distinction prevents a hard regional label from being mistaken for the probability model itself.

Four strongly coupled, point-compatible extensions are registered. First, GB--Logistic--PP replaces an unpooled local residual by a common shrinkage prior on $(b_j,u_j)$; the precision of this prior is a monotone function of effective support, ball-wise heterogeneity, posterior width and boundary margin. Second, GB--Beta--Binomial--S retains the conditional point surface and places a local probability/variance layer around it,
\[
 \mu_j(z)=\sigma\!\left(\eta_{\rm point}(z)+g_j\gamma_jr_j(z)\right),\qquad
 \Theta_j(z)\mid z\sim\operatorname{Beta}\!\left(\kappa_j(z)\mu_j(z),\kappa_j(z)[1-\mu_j(z)]\right),
\]
with $Y_i\mid\Theta_j(z_i)\sim\operatorname{Bernoulli}(\Theta_j(z_i))$. Thus the layer represents local posterior width, overdispersion and shrinkage; it does not replace $\mu_j(z)$ by a constant cell probability. Third, GB--cloglog/GB--Hazard--S uses
\[
 p_{j,h}(z)=1-\exp\!\left[-\Delta_h\exp\{\eta_{{\rm point},h}(z)+g_{j,h}\gamma_{j,h}r_{j,h}(z)\}\right]
\]
with a declared horizon-order constraint and time-block calibration. Fourth, GB--Dirichlet--S extends the same construction to a probability vector,
\[
 \boldsymbol\pi_j(z)\sim\operatorname{Dirichlet}\!\left(\kappa_j(z)\boldsymbol\mu_j(z)\right),
 \qquad \boldsymbol\mu_j(z)=\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\boldsymbol\eta_{\rm point}(z)+g_j\boldsymbol\gamma_j\odot\boldsymbol r_j(z)\right),
\]
for multiclass group probabilities. In both cases, zero local effects or a failed gate returns the corresponding point surface (logistic, hazard or softmax).

\subsection{Support-adaptive partial pooling and selective local experts}
\label{sec:partial_pooling_local_experts}
The partially pooled GB--Logistic family is the preferred next regional head because it preserves the exact point fallback while controlling sparse-cell variance. Let $A_j$ be a frozen parent cell, $c_j$ its centre and $z=\phi(x)$. We write
\[
 \eta_j(x)=\eta_{\rm point}(x)+\gamma_j\,r_j(z),\qquad
 r_j(z)=b_j+u_j^\top(z-c_j),
\]
where $(b_j,u_j)$ are estimated with a common shrinkage prior and $\gamma_j\in[0,1]$ is determined before the outer test from cross-fitted evidence. A useful prior precision is a monotone function of class-balanced/dependence-adjusted effective support $s_j$, posterior width $w_j$, residual heterogeneity $\rho_j$ and the exact allocation margin $m_j$:
\[
 \gamma_j=\frac{s_j\,\rho_j\,m_j}{s_j\,\rho_j\,m_j+\lambda(1+w_j)},
 \qquad \gamma_j=0\ \text{if any support, width or margin certificate fails}.
\]
The global hyperparameter $\lambda$ is estimated only on the calibration role; the local coefficients are fit on the estimation role with HARPNUM-grouped cross-fitting, and no outer-test label enters either quantity. The expression is a finite-sample shrinkage rule, not a claim that these diagnostics identify the optimal prior. It makes the nesting explicit: $\gamma_j=0$ gives the point Logistic head exactly, and a low-support cell cannot gain a large local correction merely because its empirical residual is large.

The selector must be risk-contribution aware rather than activate every parent. On the calibration role we predeclare a finite menu of $\lambda$, a maximum activation fraction (at most 15--20\%), a minimum number of independent HARPNUM groups, and a one-sided upper-confidence bound for the cell-level point-regret reduction. The activation score combines estimated risk contribution, residual heterogeneity and effective support; it is never ranked with outer-test labels. A cell is opened only when its cross-fitted regret upper bound is below zero, its support and boundary margins pass, and the candidate remains non-inferior after the simultaneous finite-menu correction. All other cells set $\gamma_j=0$ and use the point surface. The current Solar OOF audit is deliberately reported as a negative control for this design: only $1/32$ cells at 24 h and $0/32$ at 48 h passed the conservative support gate, and the global paired Brier interval crossed zero. Thus the protocol is retained as an auditable selective-expert route, not as a promoted regional precision result.

A separate implementation screen made this route concrete without changing the allocation. It fixed $K=32$, $n_{\rm init}=1$ and the physical point anchor, opened at most 4/6/8/10 cells (12.5--31.25\%), and multiplied each opened local block by the structure-role positive-count factor $n_{+,j}/(n_{+,j}+\tau)$ with $\tau\in\{0,2,4,8,16\}$. The cell gate used calibration-role class-count and positive-count floors; all coefficients and $C$ values were refit before the single outer test read. In this exploratory role-specific run, the predeclared four-cell route changed Brier relative to its same-run point anchor by $-0.000138[-0.000730,+0.000377]$ at 24 h and $-0.000217[-0.000619,+0.000207]$ at 48 h. It was nevertheless worse than the corresponding all-cell $S$ head at 24 h ($+0.000145[-0.000185,+0.000503]$) and 48 h ($+0.000124[-0.000566,+0.000792]$), while the rare-event factor produced no detectable change across this grid. The candidate grid has no simultaneous two-horizon non-inferiority certificate, and the strict one-sided regret-UCB gate was not yet applied, so these numbers are a screen rather than a new GB--Logistic gain. The class-balanced selective protocol therefore remains SI-only; raw-support-only improvements are excluded because their selected rows were zero-positive cells. The complete cell scores, gate table, predictions, bootstrap intervals and hashes are in \texttt{evidence/next\_upgrade/solar\_accuracy\_upgrade/selective\_s/} and the executable is \texttt{run\_selective\_s\_rare\_shrink.py}.

We then ran the stricter finite-menu $S_{\rm sel}$ screen with the allocation held fixed ($K=32$, $n_{\rm init}=1$, unchanged centres/radii/tie rule). The screen regenerated only the local head, cell gate, predictions and HARPNUM-grouped bootstrap; it did not create a new allocation version or a new outer-time certificate. The fixed policy activated 5 cells (16.8\% of test rows) at 24 h and 6 cells (22.8\% of test rows) at 48 h. These are local-head activation fractions, not saved point forward calls: the full point surface and feature matrix are still evaluated. Relative to its matched point head, Brier changes were $-0.000253[-0.000655,+0.000113]$ and $-0.000191[-0.000685,+0.000292]$; relative to canonical M4 they were $+0.000031[-0.000510,+0.000712]$ and $+0.000150[-0.000378,+0.000753]$. Log-loss and ECE intervals also crossed zero. Thus $S_{\rm sel}$ remains a controlled refinement with exact point fallback and no confirmed precision gain over either the matched point or all-cell M4. Its machine-readable outputs are \texttt{evidence/next\_upgrade/solar\_accuracy\_upgrade/selective\_s\_bootstrap.csv}, \texttt{selective\_s\_candidate\_grid.csv}, \texttt{selective\_s\_cell\_stats.csv} and \texttt{selective\_s\_predictions.csv}. If a future candidate changes centres, radii, metric, tie rule or assignments, the complete support, boundary, failure, reuse and action fingerprints must be regenerated before reuse.

\subsection{Finite candidate menu for joint centre--radius--Logistic updates}
\label{sec:finite_joint_geometry_menu}
Jointly updating centres, radii and the Logistic boundary is treated as a finite model-selection problem. A candidate is indexed by
\[
 g=(\delta_c,\,\kappa_r,\,C,\,M,\,\mathsf{soft}),
\]
where $\delta_c$ is a bounded centre displacement, $\kappa_r$ is a radius multiplier, $C$ is the Logistic regularisation, $M$ is an optional bounded metric/weight choice and $\mathsf{soft}$ records whether a continuous basis is used only for prediction. A predeclared Solar menu is
\[
 \delta_c\in\{0,\pm0.1,\pm0.2\}\;\mathrm{SD},\quad
 \kappa_r\in\{0.9,1.0,1.1\},\quad
 C\in\{0.01,0.03,0.1\},
\]
with a maximum of 24 complete candidates after removing duplicate fingerprints. Centre and radius proposals are generated on the structure role; the Logistic head is fitted on the estimation role; calibration/validation selects one candidate using Brier, log loss, ECE, support, radial margin, Voronoi margin and complexity. No continuous optimizer is used to select the deployed candidate.

After selection, the complete geometry is rebuilt. The assignment map, tie rule, centre and radius arrays, support counts, radial margins, Voronoi margins, metric fingerprint, probability-head fingerprint and deployment-measure identifier are frozen together. Any centre or radius change invalidates the old geometry and action fingerprints; cached support, boundary and action records cannot be reused. A candidate fails closed to the point head when optimization or menu evaluation is incomplete, assignment change exceeds its trust-region bound, any required support lower bound fails, a radial/Voronoi margin is negative, or the paired Brier/ECE gate is not met. The regional action integral is evaluated separately with the declared deployment measure and is never broadcast to sample rows.

The existing continuous trust-region probe is retained as a failure-boundary control. Its optimizer stopped at the iteration limit at both horizons; the unreturned soft probe changed Brier by $+0.000037[-0.000452,+0.000519]$ and $+0.000177[-0.000290,+0.000615]$ at 24/48 h, while the strict fail-closed contract returned exactly to point Logistic. The alternating centre--radius probe improved the historical point anchor but was indistinguishable from canonical M4 and produced no safe regional route. These results justify replacing continuous joint optimization by the finite menu above; they do not support a joint-geometry accuracy claim. Machine-readable fields and hashes are in \texttt{evidence/next\_upgrade/joint\_geometry\_probe/FINITE\_MENU\_JOINT\_GEOMETRY\_PROTOCOL\_CN.md}, \texttt{finite\_menu\_joint\_geometry\_protocol.json} and the existing joint-probe tables.

\paragraph{Temporal base-rate adaptation remains exploratory.}
The Solar test stream shows a large change in observed event prevalence across chronological blocks, but an oracle year-intercept diagnostic gives only a small upper-bound improvement. A future online protocol may update only a horizon-by-time intercept, $b_{h,t}=b_{h,0}+\operatorname{logit}(\widehat\pi_{h,t})-\operatorname{logit}(\widehat\pi_{h,0})$, using labels that have matured before time $t$; the physical representation, allocation, local coefficients and action fingerprints remain frozen. If the lagged prevalence estimate or overlap certificate fails, the intercept and point route revert to their frozen values. This is a temporal stability experiment, not a current accuracy promotion.


This hierarchy also separates the estimands. Sample-level Brier is evaluated on $\widehat q_A(x)$, whereas a region-level action targets $\theta_{A,D}$ and reports $\widehat\theta_{A,D}$ with its uncertainty radius. A constant region posterior is not accepted as a substitute for the conditional surface when within-region heterogeneity is non-negligible. Any future precision claim requires a paired grouped-bootstrap interval on the appropriate estimand, no ECE deterioration beyond tolerance, and one outer time-block evaluation.

\paragraph{Surface--action gate audit.}
A lightweight calibration-only gate was then applied to the frozen physical-compact $K=32$ surface. It required effective support at least 60, a Beta$(1,1)$ posterior 95\% width no larger than 0.08, and a within-region heterogeneity score (point-probability spread or residual standard deviation) no larger than 0.05. The gate was constructed without test labels. At 24/48 h it activated 47.7\%/39.0\% of test rows; the sample-level surface Brier changed from $0.016645/0.024648$ to $0.016651/0.024654$, with paired grouped changes $+0.000006[-0.000011,+0.000032]$ and $+0.000006[-0.000001,+0.000020]$. Thus the gate protects the point fallback but supplies no confirmed sample-level gain. For the separate region-action diagnostic, the active-region equal-cell rate-MSE was $3.22\times10^{-6}/3.16\times10^{-7}$ and the test-mass-weighted rate-MSE was $2.70\times10^{-6}/2.34\times10^{-7}$ at 24/48 h. These are squared errors between a pooled cell prevalence and its pooled test prevalence; they are not binary-event Brier scores or a full deployment-action score. The active cells are selected for low heterogeneity and are not representative of the full test population. A genuine action score must be defined per region--anchor/window unit or by a declared fixed-budget utility. The result is a safety and estimand-separation audit, not an accuracy promotion. The machine-readable files are \texttt{surface\_action\_gate\_*.csv/json}, \texttt{run\_surface\_action\_gate\_audit.py} and \texttt{README\_SURFACE\_ACTION\_GATE\_CN.md}.

\paragraph{Outer-time HARPNUM--window deployment estimand and DR protocol.}
For the Solar action task, one deployment observation is indexed by $(h,\mathrm{HARPNUM},t_a)$, where $t_a$ is the frozen 12-h anchor time and $h\in\{24,48\}$ is the forecast horizon. Its label is $Y_{h,g}=1$ if the first eligible M1.0-or-higher event associated with the same HARPNUM occurs strictly after $t_a$ and no later than $t_a+h$; duplicate anchors, overlapping windows and incomplete follow-up are resolved before any split. A region-level action estimand is then
\[
 \theta_{A,D}^{(h)}=\mathbb E_{D}\!\left[m_D^{(h)}(X)\mid X\in A\right]
 =\int_A m_D^{(h)}(x)\,d\mu_{A,D}^{\rm deploy}(x),
\]
where $D$ is a declared target time window or target group measure, not the source row measure. The planned outer-time analysis fits the physical point head, density ratio, local residual and support prior only inside an earlier development/calibration interval; it freezes the allocation, tie rule, labels and ratio clipping; and it evaluates the resulting $\widehat q_A$ and $\widehat\theta_{A,D}$ once on a later, genuinely unseen time block. The target block is not used to choose $K$, geometry, residual strength, gate thresholds or routing.

The primary regional estimator is a HARPNUM/time cross-fitted doubly robust integral,
\[
 \widehat\theta_{A,D}^{\rm DR}=
 \frac{1}{|T_A|}\sum_{j\in T_A}\widehat m(X_j)
 +\frac{\sum_{i\in S_A}\widehat w(X_i)\{Y_i-\widehat m(X_i)\}}
 {\sum_{i\in S_A}\widehat w(X_i)},
\]
with explicit overlap, density-ratio clipping, effective support and target-label provenance. Influence contributions are resampled by HARPNUM and time block. A candidate is promotable only if the sample-level surface passes its Brier/ECE gate, the regional action score or fixed-budget utility is reported on the separate estimand, and the DR interval is non-inferior to the matched point action under the outer block. The released 2019--2024 endpoint currently lacks the required labels and raw reconstruction, so this outer-time DR result is not claimed in the present manuscript; the machine-readable protocol is \texttt{outer\_time\_deployment\_protocol.json} with its README.
